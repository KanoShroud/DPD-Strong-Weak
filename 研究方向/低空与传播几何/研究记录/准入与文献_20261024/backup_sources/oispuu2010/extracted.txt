This paper investigates the direct position determination (DPD) problem from passive
measurements made with a moving antenna array in the case of a time-varying number
of emitting sources. We derive the Crame´r-Rao bound (CRB) for the estimation problem
and ﬁnd an approximation that is applicable for a large number of observations. We use
different DPD approaches to solve the estimation problem. Therein, all source positions
are estimated directly from a single cost function that results from fusing all
measurements. The considered DPD approaches are based on Capon’s method and the
maximum likelihood (ML) estimation. The ML approach offers a superior performance
compared to the Capon-type approach, but leads to a high-dimensional optimization.
We use the alternating projection technique to solve the high-dimensional optimization
by a sequence of low-dimensional optimizations. Finally, we propose an iterative direct
detection and position determination (DDPD) approach that combines the aforemen-
tioned DPD and the determination of the total number of sources (commonly called
detection), because the total number of sources is typically unknown. We present
simulation results that demonstrate the performance of the DDPD approach.

1. Introduction
Localization of multiple sources using passive sensor
arrays is a fundamental task encountered in various
ﬁelds like wireless communication, radar, sonar, radio
astronomy, and seismology. Passive bearings-only locali-
zation (BOL) can be realized by multiple sensors, a moving
sensor, or both. A moving platform on a known path is the
preferable solution in many applications. The sensor
is typically airborne e.g. on an aircraft, a helicopter, or a
drone. Also of special importance is underwater acoustic
localization using hydrophone arrays on a submarine.
We consider a scenario with N observations of Q
stationary narrowband far-ﬁeld sources. An array delivers
a batch of data snapshots (complex output data vectors)
with a sufﬁciently high data rate, i.e. the scenario is
assumed
to
be
stationary
during
one
batch
and
non-stationary from batch to batch.
The traditional approach to solve the localization
problem consists of four steps (Fig. 1). After some
pre-processing of the sensor data, the directions of
arrival (DOAs) of all sources at all measurement points
are estimated with a direction ﬁnding (DF) algorithm, like
the subspace-based multiple signal classiﬁcation (MUSIC)
method. Typically, DF systems report measurements of
different
origin,
e.g.
true
sources
and
clutter
(false
alarms). Then a bearing data association step follows to
partition the DOAs into sets of DOAs belonging to the
same source. Multiple hypotheses tracking (MHT) is
generally accepted as the preferred method for solving
the measurement-to-track association problem [2]. In the
last step, the DOAs for each source are used to estimate its
position with the help of a suitable BOL algorithm, e.g. the
bearings can be intersected to determine the emitter

Recently, some direct position determination (DPD)

the case of multiple sources. The cyclic DPD algorithm for

In all these DPD approaches, the parameters of interest
are obtained by minimizing a single cost function into

estimation accuracy of the source state obtained in this
way is much better than the traditional localization

bound (CRB).
However, the methods presented so far only investi-
gate the case where the number of emitting sources is
constant. Farina et al. derived the CRB for the general case
that the probability of detection is smaller than unity [11].
We extend the results to the case of multiple sources with
intermittent emission and adapt them to derive the CRB
for the DPD problem. We present two DPD approaches
based on Capon’s (CPN) method [12] and deterministic
ML [13]. We show that the positioning accuracy of the
ML-DPD approach is much better compared to the
CPN-DPD
approach
in
situations
of
a
time-varying
number of emitting sources, but this approach leads to a
high-dimensional optimization problem. We use the
alternating projection (AP) technique to solve the high-
dimensional optimization by a sequence of low-dimen-
sional
optimizations
similar
to
results
in
[14].
The
considered DPD approaches require knowledge of the
total number of sources, but in practice this number is
unknown. We propose an iterative solution that combines
the DPD and the determination of the total number of
sources. In the area of signal processing this is commonly
referred to as detection. This direct detection and position
determination (DDPD) approach uses a sequence of
statistical tests to decide if the choice of the source
number is correct. The basis of the tests is the fact that the
optima of the ML-DPD cost function are w2- distributed.
We present simulation results that demonstrate the
performance of the DDPD approach.
This paper is organized as follows: Section 2.1 intro-
duces the concept of emitting/non-emitting sequences,
Section 2.2 presents the data model, and Section 2.3
formulates the estimation problem. In Section 2.4 we
derive the CRB for the described DPD problem and ﬁnd an
approximation which is applicable for practical purposes.
In Section 3.1, we outline the considered DPD approaches,
in Section 3.2 we compare the DPD approaches in
simulations and investigate the performance of the DPD
approaches. In Section 3.3 we propose the novel DDPD
approach. The conclusions are given in Section 4.

3.1. DPD approaches
In this section we outline the DPD approaches (Fig. 2)
to solve the localization problem with the assumption
that the number of sources Q is known. Note that the
proposed DPD approaches do not account for the emitting
probabilities Pe,q, because they are not sensor parameters
like the probability of detection.
The considered DPD approaches are based on Capon’s
method and ML estimation using a low-dimensional or
high-dimensional optimization, respectively. Therein, the
source positions are calculated directly in one step from
the covariance matrices at all sensor positions. We
mention that, alternatively, the corresponding subspace
data can be used by a MUSIC-type or SSF-type (subspace
ﬁtting) approach [1]. It is well-known that MUSIC gives
superior resolution and tends to be power independent in
comparison to Capon’s method, but it is important to note
that DPD approaches based on the subspaces require the
knowledge of the effective number of sources per batch
Qn, n=1,y,N, (Assumption A4), while DPD approaches
based on the covariance matrices do not need any a priori
knowledge
about
the
effective
number
of
emitting
sources.

4. Conclusions
We investigated the DPD problem for multiple sources
with intermittent emission. Therefore, we derived the
deterministic
CRB
and
presented
a
computationally
convenient approximation of the CRB, which is applicable
for practical purposes. Furthermore, we proposed DPD
approaches based on the data covariance matrices using a
low- or high-dimensional optimization. We used the AP
technique to solve the high-dimensional optimization
by a sequence of low-dimensional optimizations. In
simulations, we demonstrated the superior performance
of the ML-DPD approach over the CPN-DPD approach.
Consequently, the ML-DPD (and AP-DPD) approach offers
a tactical advantage for locating sources with intermittent
emission. We proposed an iterative DDPD approach to
determine the number of sources and the corresponding
source locations. We investigated the proposed method in
simulations. Note that the approaches can be extended to
moving sources.
