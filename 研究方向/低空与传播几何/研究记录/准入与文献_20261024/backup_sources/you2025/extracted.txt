A B S T R A C T
This paper proposes a direct position determination (DPD) method for stationary and moving non-cooperative 
sources. Employing an unbalanced group of measurements consisting of uncompressed measurements at the 
central receiver and compressed measurements from all other auxiliary receivers, the method estimates the 
source position directly in the hybrid measurement domain without original signal recovering, where the 
compressing matrix is not restricted to any specific form. A block coordinate descent (BCD)-like iterative 
algorithm is proposed to handle the high-dimensional optimization problem of joint position and velocity 
estimation for moving emitters, where a generalized cross ambiguity function (GCAF) is proposed to extract 
the time-differences-of-arrival (TDOA) parameters from the hybrid measurements to initialize the iteration 
process. In addition, the hybrid measurements-based Cramér–Rao lower bound (CRLB) for emitter position is 
derived for performance evaluation. Several numerical case studies are carried out to evaluate the effectiveness 
of the proposed DPD method as well as the proposed GCAF. The proposed method is expected to extend the 
applicability of compressive sensing (CS)-based DPD to cases where there is relative radial motion between 
the source.
1. Introduction
Emmiter localization through a sensor network is of vital signif-
icance for many applications such as emergency rescue, cognitive 
communications and military decision support [1–8]. Classical passive 
localization methods are usually performed in a two-step manner. In 
the first step, position related parameters are estimated based on the 
received signals. Then in the second step the emitter’s location is 
estimated by a central receiver which collects all necessary parameters. 
As widely recognized, the two-step approaches usually perform well 
at high signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs), but their performance degrade 
greatly when SNR becomes low [2]. To improve the localization perfor-
mance at low SNRs, direct position determination (DPD) is introduced 
which estimates the source location directly from the received sig-
nals [9–15] without the step of parameter extraction. DPD methods 
are proved to outperform the two-step counterparts by utilizing the 
underlying constraint that all received signals come from a common 
position [1], which is simply ignored in two-step methods
However, the advantages of DPD approaches are obtained at the 
cost of huge data interaction, which is not desirable in many wireless 
communication based applications such as unmanned vehicle (UAV)

methods, among which compressive sensing (CS) techniques are usually 
considered [16–18].
A CS method typically consists of a signal compression step and 
a signal reconstruction step [19]. Generally, the signal reconstruction 
step requires great computation effort [20–22] and is subject to large 
reconstruction error when SNR is low. In [23], a CS based method 
is proposed for TDOA estimation without signal reconstruction by 
employing the special property of a Hadamard matrix. The approach 
is shown to outperform the orthogonal matching pursuit (OMP) recon-
struction method [24] and the focal underdetermined system solver 
(FOCUSS) reconstruction method [25] regarding parameter estimation 
accuracy and computational complexity.
Despite the works on CS based data compression for classical two-
step methods, very few data compression results have been reported for 
DPD methods. In [26], we propose a reconstruction free DPD method 
which explores the time-differences-of-arrival (TDOA) among receivers 
by showing that the time-shift relationship can be retained when the

DPD approach of narrowband signals has been proposed for stationary

In practice, it is common to locate an emitter using moving plat-
forms such as unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), trucks and satellites. 
However, when there are relative radial motion between the source 
and the receiver, i.e., when frequency-differences-of-arrival (FDOA) is 
present, it is straightforward to show that the time-shift and frequency-
shift relationship among receivers will be broken even Hadamard ma-
trix is used, making it infeasible to extend to the case of moving 
platforms based localization simply following the existing solutions 
in [19,26].
To address the problem of CS based DPD when relative radial 
motion is present, in this paper, a DPD method is proposed to locate 
stationary and moving emitters, based on unbalanced groups of hybrid 
measurements consisting of original uncompressed measurements at 
the central receiver and compressed measurements from all other auxil-
iary receivers. The proposed DPD algorithm does not seek to recover the 
original signal received by auxiliary receivers, but to perform DPD di-
rectly based on the hybrid measurements. The performance of proposed 
algorithm is verified via simulation experiments. Main contributions of 
the current work include:
(i) A CS based DPD method is outlined for moving platforms by 
reconstructing the compressed measurements of the auxiliary receivers 
from the uncompressed measurements at the central receiver with-
out increasing the communication bandwidth, while the compressing 
matrix is not restricted to any specific form.
(ii) A block coordinate decent (BCD) like iterative algorithm is pro-
posed to solve the high-dimensional optimization problem of moving 
emitters, where a generalized cross ambiguity function named GCAF is 
proposed to extract the TDOAs to initialize the iteration. The covariance 
of GCAF based TDOAs is derived in detail.
(iii) Cramér–Rao lower bounds (CRLBs) for position estimation are 
derived for the case of stationary and moving emitters using hybrid 
measurements.
The reminder of the paper is organized as follows: Section 2 intro-
duces the measurement models and the relevant symbols and notations. 
Section 3 outlines the DPD approach for stationary and moving sources, 
and details the proposed GCAF and the iterative algorithm for high-
dimensional optimization of moving sources. CRLBs for emitter position 
are given in Section 6. Section 5 conducts several numerical case stud-
ies to investigate the performance of the proposed approach. Finally, 
Section 6 concludes the paper.

3.1. DPD framework

the time-shift relationship will be broken by the compressing operation.

Hadamard compressing matrix is used, as shown in detail in Appendix

seems infeasible when 𝑓𝑑𝑙≠0. This is attributed to the fact that the

6. Conclusion
This paper develops a DPD method for stationary and moving 
non-cooperative emitters using moving receivers and interactive data 
compression. Using an unbalanced group of measurements consisting of 
original uncompressed measurements at the central receiver and com-
pressed measurements from all other auxiliary receivers, the method 
can estimate the source location directly in the hybrid measurement 
domain. An iterative algorithm is proposed to reduce the computational 
complexity of the high-dimensional optimization problem of moving 
emitter localization, while a GCAF is proposed to extract the TDOA 
parameters to initialize the iteration. The CRLBs for emitter position 
using hybrid measurements are derived for performance evaluation. 
Fig. 10. RMS localization error of the proposed DPD method when the path loss is 
unknown.
The proposed DPD method performs comparably with the classical 
DPD method without CS at high SNRs. With a reasonable compressing 
rate, the CS-based DPD method significantly outperforms the classical 
two-step methods without CS in low SNR scenarios.
While the existing works on CS-based DPD approach only consider 
localization of stationary emitters using Hadamard compressing matrix 
which is only applicable for data length of 2𝑛 where 𝑛 is a positive 
integer, this paper extends the methodology in three aspects: Firstly, it 
extends the applicability of CS-based DPD approach for stationary emit-
ters to the case when there is relative radial motion between the source 
and the receiver. Secondly, it extends the applicability of CS-based DPD 
approach to moving emitters. And thirdly, the compressing matrix is
