Decentralized Indoor Direct Localization With
Multiple Wi-Fi Access Points
Ziqiang Wang , Member, IEEE, Bo Tan , Member, IEEE, Mikko Valkama , Fellow, IEEE,
Lei Xie , Member, IEEE, and Qun Wan , Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—In this paper, a decentralized iterative maximum
likelihood (ML) direct position determination (DIM-DPD) algo-
rithm is proposed based on the expectation maximization (EM)
concept for user equipment (UE) localization in Wi-Fi systems.
By innovatively treating the non-line-of-sight (NLoS) angles-of-
arrival (AoAs) and observed times-of-arrival (ToAs) as nuisance
parameters in the received signal model and parameter esti-
mation procedure, the proposed DIM-DPD demonstrates its
adaptability and localization efﬁciency in dense multipath indoor
environments. In the proposed method, the position of the UE
is incorporated in the vectors denoting the location differences
between the UE and the access point (APs), referred to as
the UE-AP location difference vectors. The set of UE-AP location
difference vectors allows constructing the related UE-AP vari-
ables, serving as the latent variables in the EM iterations. Then,
by taking advantage of the alternating projection technique,
the nuisance parameters and UE-AP variables in the DIM-
DPD algorithm are alternatively updated on separated APs in
parallel. Furthermore, instead of traditional grid search, the
UE position is updated with an efﬁcient closed-form solution
by aggregating the distributed estimated low-dimensional UE-
AP variables. Thus, overall, the proposed DIM-DPD approach
facilitates decentralized direct localization with implementa-
tion feasible processing complexity. The provided numerical
simulation results demonstrate that the proposed DIM-DPD
algorithm achieves high positioning accuracy, fast convergence,
and a good balance between computational complexity and
performance.
Index Terms—Direct position determination (DPD), expecta-
tion maximization (EM), decentralization, alternating projection,
multipath, indoor localization, Wi-Fi.

The activation of wideband and large aperture millimeter
wave (mmWave) transceivers provide large expectations on
precise range and angular resolutions [5], [6]. However, factors
such as high cost [7] and intermittent blockage caused by
narrow beam coverage [8] may hinder the popularity of the
mmWave localization systems. Therefore, the Wi-Fi-based 2.4
or 5.8 GHz localization systems remain widely used and hold
also promise for further exploration [9], [10] – particularly
indoors – despite the intensive studies in the past two decades
[11], [12], [13]. In many existing works, the received signal
strength indicator (RSSI) is a popular indicator to estimate the
location of user equipment (UE) using a pathloss model [14]
or utilizing ﬁngerprint-based approaches [15]. Unfortunately,
pathloss-based approaches only achieve coarse localization
due to the intractable environmental factors that affect the
RSSI while the ﬁngerprint-based methods require additional
server infrastructure and costly data collection campaigns.
Also, both the pathloss- and ﬁngerprint-based approaches
show low adaptability to environmental changes. In addition
to RSSI, the channel state information (CSI) [16], [17], [18]
of the Wi-Fi devices provides another opportunity to estimate
ﬁne-grained information, for example, time-of-arrival (ToA)
or time-difference-of-arrival (TDoA) for localization. How-
ever, due to the timing offsets between the digital-to-analog
converter (DAC) of the access point (AP) and the analog-
to-digital converter (ADC) of the UE, the direct estimation
of ToA based on commodity Wi-Fi devices does not rep-
resent the signal propagation distance [19], [20]. Therefore,
additional procedures, such as time-of-ﬂight (ToF) sanitization
or the Fine Timing Measurement (FTM) protocol [21], are
commonly required to eliminate excessive delays and biases
before using the ToA estimation for positioning.
In addition to ToA, the angle-of-arrival (AoA) is another
important feature for localization due to the wide-scale adop-
tion of multiple antennas in commodity Wi-Fi devices and
the relaxed synchronization requirements between the UE and
the APs. The work in [22] proved that sub-meter precision
can be achieved using up to six APs that are equipped with
eight-element antenna arrays in indoor multipath scenarios.
In practice, it is not easy to achieve similar accuracy with
commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) devices (equipped typically
with three or four antenna elements), particularly in a dense
indoor environment where the number of non-line-of-sight
(NLoS) multipaths is difﬁcult to resolve by a limited number
of antennas. To this end, the researchers in [23], [24] and

to separate the line-of-sight (LoS) path and the NLoS paths

steps: ﬁrst, to estimate intermediate parameters such as ToA
and AoA on different APs; second, to aggregate intermediate
parameters for position calculation. Since APs estimate inter-
mediate parameters independently, such two-step approaches
neglect the constraint that all parameters should be estimated
from observations from the same signal source [26]. Extra data
association will be needed to maintain performance, especially
in a low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) scenario. Therefore, the
technology of one-step direct position determination (DPD)
has drawn increasing attention recently. By modeling the
source position as vector parameter in the received signal
model, DPD turns the positioning into a parameter estimation
problem [27].
A. State-of-the-Art in DPD Methods
DPD is a generic method for different hardware or system
setups such as the number of antennas, bandwidth, and number
of subcarriers. To this end, array-based DPD methods have
been studied extensively [28], [29], [30]. An early study
proposed a maximum likelihood DPD (ML-DPD) algorithm
for a single stationary source [31], followed by a fast Fourier
transform (FFT)-based implementation variant [32]. The DPD
study was then extended to multiple sources in [33], which
reached a balance between complexity and precision using the
subspace data fusion (SDF) DPD algorithm, however, requir-
ing prior knowledge of the number of sources. Then, a DPD
algorithm derived from the distortionless minimum-variance
response (MVDR) in [34] and [35] further excludes the need
for the number of sources. Except for the computationally
heavy and complex ML-DPD, the aforementioned methods
experience a signiﬁcant performance drop in the low SNR
regions as a trade-off for computing efﬁciency. Thus, recently,
importance sampling (IS) was introduced in DPD processing
context in [36] and [37] to trade-off between complexity and
accuracy over a wide SNR range.
Though designed for the LoS environment, DPD methods
are often capable for localizing a signal source in multipath
environments – however, with limited performance in the
dense multipath scenario due to the limited capacity to handle
the resulting energy dispersion. Thus, it becomes necessary to
model the NLoS paths in the received signal for improving
the DPD performance in dense multipath environments. In
[38], a variant of the ML-DPD algorithm was proposed for
the dense multipath scenario with the requirement of statistical
multipath information. An SDF-based algorithm called multi-
path scenario DPD (SM-DPD) was proposed for a multipath
environment in [39], with a decrease in localization accuracy
as a trade-off for computational simplicity. In [40], DPD was
modeled as a sparse signal recovery problem in the dense
multipath environment to eliminate the need for statistical
channel information in ML based DPD methods. The work
was extended to the MIMO conﬁguration in [41], however,
leading to a second-order cone programming (SOCP) convex
problem that results in high computational complexity. Recent
work in [42] proved that grid search is an effective means
of ensuring fast convergence and global optimality for sparse
DPD algorithms. However, choosing the grid size is always
a dilemma between complexity and accuracy. Furthermore,
when summarizing DPD algorithms for dense multipath envi-
ronments, one can observe that ToA (or more generally, timing
information) is commonly neglected as an ingredient in the
received signal models.
In addition to accuracy and complexity concerns, the possi-
ble implementation constraints form another factor that hinders
the popularity of DPD. To date, most DPD methods require
centralized implementation due to the need to aggregate radio
signal samples from different APs to execute estimation and
other processing algorithms. This requirement imposes addi-
tional investments in the servers and network infrastructure
while also resulting into extra network trafﬁc to convey data
from the APs to the central server. Even with the advantages
of high accuracy and suitability for dense multipath environ-
ments, high cost and inefﬁcient network utilization still hinder
the popularity of DPD methods [43], [44] in practice.
B. Proposed Decentralized Maximum Likelihood DPD
Method
In this paper, motivated by the above theoretical and prac-
tical analysis of state-of-the-art DPD methods, we propose
a decentralized DPD algorithm with modest computational
load and adaptivity in high-density multipath environments.
The considered scenario includes a single stationary UE
under position estimation and multiple asynchronous station-
ary commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) Wi-Fi APs with the
typical 4-antenna conﬁguration (as deﬁned in IEEE 802.11ac
standard). To this end, a decentralized iterative ML DPD
algorithm (DIM-DPD) is proposed based on the iterative
expectation maximization (EM) technique [45]. In particu-
lar, the position of the UE is incorporated into the speciﬁc
UE-AP location difference vectors based on which the UE-AP
variables are constructed as latent variables and estimated
using the Laplace approximation technique. Thus, a speciﬁc
UE-AP variable and the corresponding nuisance parameters,
i.e., AoAs of the NLoS paths and the observed ToAs of all
paths, can be estimated by multiple line searches with the
alternating projection technique, and further implemented in
parallel in each AP. That is, the traditional multi-dimensional
search involving grid points is avoided.
Furthermore, the estimation of the UE position is updated
via the aggregation of low-dimensional UE-AP variables,
allowing for a closed-from solution rather than grid searching
method. Thus, the aggregation and processing are simple
enough to be implemented in light-weight UE itself rather
than at an extra central server. Finally, k-means clustering
and multiple initializations are used to ensure the global opti-
mality of ﬁnal position estimation. The provided simulation
results demonstrate that the proposed DIM-DPD outperforms
the existing baseline solvers even in decentralized manner
by comprising the observed ToA in the received signal
model and by harnessing the proposed parameter estimating
procedure.

In general, the proposed decentralized DIM-DPD approach
differentiates from the other existing DPD methods in the
following aspects: (i) The method is free from the requirement
of any dedicated server for aggregating intermediate estimates
and for ﬁnal position determination; (ii) The method allows
saving network trafﬁc which is classically used for transmitting
the raw samples between the central server and APs; (iii) The
method avoids leaking or disclosing the position information
which is usually consider private and sensitive information, by
using directly the UE rather than any dedicated and external
server to determine the UE position.
The contributions, novelty and advantages of the methods
proposed in this paper can be summarized as follows:
1) A computationally efﬁcient iterative DPD algorithm is
proposed for UE positioning in dense multipath indoor
scenarios. On the AP side, the efﬁciency is achieved by
multiple line searching which is activated by alternating
projection technique. On the UE side, a closed-form
UE-AP variable aggregation and estimation method is
proposed and deployed to avoid the burdensome posi-
tion grid search for the ﬁnal determination of the UE
position.
2) Decentralized DPD implementation is manifested by
two enablers. First, all searches can be implemented
on separate APs in parallel by choosing UE-AP loca-
tion difference vectors to construct latent variables in
the expectation step of EM iteration. Second, closed-
form aggregation of low-dimension UE-AP vectors
(rather than complete CSI data) signiﬁcantly reduces
the computational load and network trafﬁc. This fact
allows avoiding centralized server processing and the
related burden on the network, both being notable chal-
lenges in traditional DPD approaches, thus allowing the
light-weight UE to directly execute the ﬁnal position
determination.
3) From a performance perspective, the proposed DIM-
DPD is shown to achieve the Cram´er-Rao lower bound
(CRLB, derived in closed form in the Appendices)
over a wide SNR range. In addition, it is shown
that incorporating the observed ToAs as parameters
into the received signal model and EM iteration can
improve the accuracy of position estimation, despite the
involved clock biases. This stems from the enhanced
ability to differentiate between the involved multipath
components.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Section II
presents the signal model and problem formulation of indoor
DPD with APs and dense multipath. The proposed algorithms
are derived and described in Section III, where the main
contributions of the paper are given. In Section IV, we analyze
and discuss the properties of the proposed DPD methods.
Numerical simulation results are presented and discussed in
Section V. Finally, conclusions are drawn in Section VI.
Selected derivations related to the involved CRLBs are pro-
vided in the Appendices.

C. Position Estimation With Blocked APs
In practice, it is very likely that the LoS path between the
UE and (some of) the APs may be completely blocked due
to the existence of obstructions in the indoor environments.

blocked ones – to implement the DPD will inevitably result in
severe position estimation bias. Thus, it is beneﬁcial to detect
CSIs from APs that contain only NLoS paths, and remove
these from the algorithm, to ensure the high accuracy of the
estimation. To this end, [44] performs detection based on the
attenuation of the path. However, attenuation is not a stable

VI. CONCLUSION
In this work, we addressed the challenges of accurate
device localization with COTS Wi-Fi devices in challenging
indoor multipath environments. A decentralized iterative DPD
algorithm, called DIM-DPD, was proposed building on the
fundamental EM estimation concepts. In each iteration cycle,
the estimation of position and nuisance parameters is sepa-
rated. The ﬁrst has a closed-form solution, while the second
is alternatively estimated. Thus, no multi-dimensional search
exists in the proposed algorithm, offering good computational
efﬁciency. Despite inevitable clock biases, the timing com-
ponents are still introduced into nuisance parameters and are
theoretically validated through CRLB, shown to be beneﬁcial
for position estimation accuracy. Moreover, only the low-
dimensional estimated UE-AP variables are collected from
APs to UE, while the communication and concentration of
complete CSIs is avoided. Simulation results show the clear
superiority of the proposed DIM-DPD over baseline prior-
art solutions in terms of position estimation accuracy and
communication resource consumption.
