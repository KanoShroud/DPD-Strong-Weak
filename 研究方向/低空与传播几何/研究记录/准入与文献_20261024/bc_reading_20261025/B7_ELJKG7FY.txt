Low-altitude communication networks are emerging to facil- 
itate reliable connectivity for aerial platforms, where a deep 
understanding of the radio propagation channel is funda- 
mental for the design and optimization of the communica- 
tion systems. In this study, a self-developed air-to-ground 
(A2G) channel sounder is employed to conduct ﬁeld mea- 
surements under three typical communication scenarios, i.e., 
sports ﬁeld, farmland, and over-water. In each scenario, chan- 
nel measurement data are collected at different ﬂight heights 
of the unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV), i.e., 10 m and 15 m. 
A dataset of measured channel impulse responses (CIRs) is 
constructed accordingly. Under each scenario, hundreds of 
CIR snapshots are recorded, where each CIR snapshot has 
a size of 1 × 200. Each CIR snapshot is also labeled with 
corresponding global positioning system (GPS) locations and 
time, where time represents the seconds elapsed since the 
start of the measurement. All data are stored in .xls format. 
Unlike most existing A2G channel measurement datasets 
that are limited to a single scenario or UAV altitude, our 
dataset simultaneously encompasses diverse scenarios and 
multiple ﬂight heights at 3.6 GHz, which enhances its value 
for reproducibility and broad applicability in future channel 
modeling and system-level evaluations. The dataset is val- 
idated through on-site monitoring and repeated measure-

1. Value of the Data 
• Due to the complexity of A2G channel measurement campaigns, the channel dataset for A2G 
communications is very rare. Our dataset provides channel measurement data at different 
heights under three typical A2G communication scenarios. It is valuable for deeply under- 
standing height- and scenario-dependent A2G channels. 
• The measured CIRs included in this dataset can be used to further extract the path loss, 
shadow fading, K-factor, delay spread, and so on. These characteristics can contribute to sup- 
porting the parameter determination and performance evaluation of the A2G channel model. 
Moreover, they are also valuable references for parameter design of the communication sys- 
tem [ 1–3 ]. 
• The radio propagation channel is a critical component of system-level simulation. This 
dataset provides realistic A2G channel data that can be used to optimize communication 
algorithms, analyze system performance, deploy the network, etc., for the low-altitude com- 
munication network [ 4–6 ].

2. Background 
Sixth generation (6 G) mobile communication systems aim to achieve full-space integrated 
communications spanning ground, air, maritime, and even space domains. Within this vision, 
A2G connectivity serves as a critical enabler for integrated space–air–ground communications in 
low-altitude airspace, and is becoming a fundamental cornerstone for constructing low-altitude 
intelligent networks. This places higher demands on the in-depth understanding of the radio 
propagation channel, where the channel measurement is the most accurate way to obtain chan- 
nel characteristics in real environments. The A2G channel measurement data can facilitate the 
development of more realistic channel models [ 2 , 3 ], and is also widely applied in various re- 
search areas such as electromagnetic environment sensing [ 7 ], and low-altitude network deploy- 
ment and optimization [ 4–6 ]. Moreover, such data are critical for validating near-ﬁeld modeling 
techniques for large-scale MIMO systems [ 8 ], beam-channel models for UAV-enabled mmWave 
communications [ 9 ], channel models involving aerial intelligent reﬂecting surfaces [ 10 ], and en- 
able performance evaluation of aerial edge computing schemes under realistic propagation con- 
ditions [ 11 ]. 
In recent years, there have been numerous studies focusing on A2G channel measurements 
[ 12 ]. For example, the authors in [ 13 ] conducted A2G channel measurements at 2.7 GHz in a ru- 
ral environment using a ﬁxed-wing UAV and analyzed path loss, fading, and delay spread char- 
acteristics. The authors in [ 1 ] conducted A2G channel measurements at 3.5 GHz in a dynamic 
campus environment using a UAV-aided real-time sounder, analyzing time-varying delay and 
Doppler characteristics. The authors in [ 14 ] proposed a UAV-based virtual array channel sound- 
ing system operating at 4.8 GHz and conducted indoor experiments to characterize the angular 
resolution performance of the A2G channel. The authors in [ 15 ] conducted A2G channel mea- 
surements at 5.8 GHz using a drone-mounted sounder in an outdoor environment to evaluate 
cell-free massive MIMO systems, focusing on spatial consistency and large-scale fading charac- 
teristics. Compared with prior A2G measurement effort s, such as [ 13 ] at 2.7 GHz focusing on a 
single suburban scenario and [ 15 ] at 5.8 GHz with limited altitude variations, the dataset pre- 
sented in this work uniquely covers multiple UAV altitudes and diverse scenarios at 3.6 GHz. 
This broader scope enhances its applicability to channel modeling and system-level evaluations 
across a wider range of 6 G low-altitude communication scenarios. Although A2G channel mea- 
surement campaigns have gained a lot of attention, open A2G channel measurement datasets 
are very rare currently, which restricts in-depth investigations of A2G channel characteristics 
and the development of high-accuracy channel models. 
To ﬁll this gap, this paper presents an A2G channel measurement dataset involving differ- 
ent UAV ﬂight altitudes and multiple typical communication scenarios. The aim is to provide 
researchers with authentic and reliable A2G measurement data for channel model development, 
system design, algorithm optimization, etc. 
3. Data Description 
Table 1 describes the structure of the main dataset. All measured raw data are stored within 
a primary directory DATASET, which contains three subdirectories corresponding to the three 
measurement scenarios: sports ﬁeld, farmland, and over-water. Each subdirectory includes two 
data ﬁles, representing the channel data acquired at two different UAV ﬂight altitudes in each 
scenario.

Before conducting the measurement campaign, it was necessary to calibrate the entire sys- 
tem to eliminate the effects of cable loss and hardware response. A back-to-back measurement 
method was employed, where the TX and RX of the channel sounding system were directly 
connected to measure the overall system loss. This measured loss was then subtracted from the 
actual results to obtain the corrected channel loss, ensuring the accuracy of CIR extraction and 
power-related parameters. 
4.2. Software setup 
The software utilized in this study is a LabVIEW-based graphical user interface (GUI), con- 
sisting of two main modules: a parameter conﬁguration module and a graphical display mod- 
ule. The parameter conﬁguration module enables real-time adjustment of RX settings, including 
measurement center frequency, sampling bandwidth, and data storage path. The graphical dis- 
play module employs built-in algorithms to process the received data in real time, extract the 
CIR, and visualize it on the user interface. This functionality facilitates the on-site evaluation 
of the validity of the acquired channel data. In practice, the LabVIEW GUI displays the CIR and 
PDP in real time, which enables researchers to assess data validity by checking the presence of 
a clear LoS peak, verifying whether the multipath distribution matched the expected environ- 
ment, and monitoring whether the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) exceeded a predeﬁned threshold. 
If anomalies were detected, such as missing peaks, unstable multipath patterns, or low SNR, the 
error was ﬁrst checked and then the corresponding measurement was immediately repeated to 
ensure that only valid data were retained in the dataset.

4.3. Measurement campaigns 
In this study, three representative scenarios are selected to perform channel measurement 
campaigns, i.e., a sports ﬁeld scenario, a farmland scenario (a typical suburban scenario), and an 
over-water scenario. The measurements are performed at 3.6 GHz, which is a typical frequency 
band for the ﬁfth-generation (5 G) communication. Table 3 presents the GPS locations of the 
ground RX in each scenario.

locations and the UAV ﬂight trajectory. The UAV takes off from the ground and ascends to an 
altitude of 10 m, then ﬂies along a predeﬁned straight trajectory. Upon reaching the endpoint, it 
ascends vertically to 15 m and returns along the same trajectory in the opposite direction. The 
channel measurement data are recorded when the UAV is ﬂying along the solid-line trajectory

5. Data Application 
Based on the measured CIRs in the dataset, we analyze key channel characteristics, including 
PDPs and PL. With the GPS locations of the ground RX and the aerial TX, the absolute propaga- 
tion delay of the LoS path is calculated based on the distance between the TX and RX. Firstly, 
Fig. 3 presents the time-varying PDPs measured under different ﬂight altitudes across the three 
scenarios. The horizontal axis represents the absolute path delay, while the vertical axis corre- 
sponds to the time of the CIR snapshot. The color scale indicates the power attenuation in dB. 
The measured time-varying PDPs reveal the multipath distribution characteristics of the 
channel throughout the entire measurement process, highlighting the relationship between mul- 
tipath delay and received power. As shown in Fig. 3 (a), two stable non-line-of-sight (NLOS) paths 
are observed in the sports ﬁeld scenario. In the farmland scenario, the presence of crops in- 
troduces a rich scattering environment, resulting in a more dispersed multipath distribution. In 
contrast, the over-water scenario exhibits signiﬁcantly fewer multipath components compared to 
the other two scenarios. Due to the scarcity of scatterers and the relatively clean environment, 
the average noise ﬂoor in the over-water scenario is noticeably lower.

6. Conclusion 
This paper presents an open A2G channel dataset collected by a self-developed channel 
sounder at 3.6 GHz under three representative scenarios: sports ﬁeld, farmland, and over-water, 
with UAV ﬂight altitudes of 10 m and 15 m. The dataset provides validated CIRs with associated 
GPS information, offering reliable measurements for analyzing low-altitude A2G propagation. Its 
contributions lie in covering multiple scenarios and altitudes, documenting detailed hardware 
and software setups for reproducibility, and including example analyses such as PDP, PL and 
RMS-DS. The dataset is openly accessible through Mendeley Data, enabling researchers to utilize 
it for channel characterization, model development, and system-level evaluation. Future work 
may extend this dataset to other frequency bands, higher UAV altitudes, and additional scenar- 
ios to further support the design and optimization of 6 G low-altitude communication networks.
