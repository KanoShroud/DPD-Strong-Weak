Abstract—Multisource localization (MSL) for overlapped sig-
nals has attracted much attention, and the existing methods
rely on the combination of multitype measurements, which puts
higher requirements on the receiver. Besides, these methods can
not avoid the problem of measurement-source association. In
view of this, on the basis of broadband signal time-frequency
spectrogram detection (TFSD), we propose an MSL scheme for
overlapped signals based on index ambiguity elimination, which
can avoid the above-mentioned measurement-source association
problem by extracting the pure part of each signal component.
Speciﬁcally, we ﬁrst conduct the time-frequency transformation
of the received signal, and analyze the overlapping types of
the time-frequency blocks (TFBs) in two aspects: 1) inter-TFB,
i.e., the overlapping types between the TFBs and 2) intra-
TFB, i.e., the overlapping types between signal components
contained in the TFB. On this basis, the TFB nonoverlapping part
extraction algorithm is designed to eliminate the overlap between
TFBs. Afterward, the signal segmentation algorithm based on
the signal characteristic change mechanism is designed to obtain
the pure part of each signal component, that is, eliminating
the index ambiguity of each signal component contained in
the extracted nonoverlapping TFB. Finally, the angle-of-arrival
(AOA) information of multiple receivers for a certain signal
can be obtained through the AOA estimation method, as well
as the location of source device corresponding to the signal
can be estimated by the triangulation method. Simulation
and experiment results verify the effectiveness of the designed
scheme.
Index Terms—Index ambiguity elimination, multisource local-
ization, overlapped signal, signal segmentation, time-frequency
spectrogram detection.

incumbent users, which makes wireless security becomes a

radiation sources can efﬁciently ﬁnd out the intruders, pro-
tecting the shared spectrum from malicious jamming or other
unauthorized utilization. In particular, accurately determining
the individual location of each signal source in overlapping
signal environments is essential for many applications, such
as wireless communication systems, autonomous vehicles,
and industrial Internet of Things (IoT) [7]. Therefore, the
multisource localization (MSL) for overlapped signals is the
focus of this article.
Over the years, a plethora of localization techniques have
been proposed by utilizing different signal or measurement
types, including ultrasound, visible light, radio frequency (RF),
inertial measurement, and hybrid signals. Among these modal-
ities, RF signals are widely used because of their ubiquity
in current wireless communication systems [8], in which the
localization methods can be categorized into geometry-based
and deep-learning (DL)-based. For geometry-based localiza-
tion, it is widely used in current communication systems.
Over the past several decades, signal metrics, such as the
received signal strength (RSS) [9], [10], time-of-arrival (TOA)
[11], [12], time-difference-of-arrival (TDOA) [13], [14], [15],
and angle-of-arrival (AOA) [16], [17], [18], [19], and their
combinations [20], [21], [22], [23] have been designed for
characterizing the location-bearing information. The essence
of RSS, TOA, TDOA, and AOA is based on measuring the
distance (distance-based) or angle (angle-based) of wireless
devices with respect to multiple receivers with known loca-
tions. By adopting trilateration or triangulation algorithms,
the location of wireless devices can be calculated from these
measurements. Techniques in geometry-based localization are
training-free, easy to analyze theoretically, and scalable to
different environments. Contrary to the practical algorithms
designed for geometry-based localization, DL-based methods
require ofﬂine training [24], [25]. Such an ofﬂine process
can sufﬁciently reduce online computations, while a large
volume of data from wireless communication systems needs
to be collected for training purposes, and the trained models
have to be updated with fresh data to adapt to environmental
variations.
Notably, MSL for overlapped signals refers to the process
of determining the individual locations of multiple sources in
an environment where their signals overlap or interfere with

each other. This is a common challenge in wireless communi-
cation systems, where multiple devices may transmit signals
simultaneously, leading to signal overlap. Compared to single-
source localization (SSL), simultaneously locating multiple
sources is more challenging in practice since the association
between measurement parameters and source devices are not
known. It is crucial to discriminate which measurements cor-
respond to the same source before localization. Moreover, the
number of possible measurement-source associations increases
exponentially with the number of receivers. Overall, MSL for
overlapped signals involves complex signal processing, data
fusion, and possibly DL to accurately determine the location
of each source in overlapping signal environments.
In view of this problem, a lot of meaningful works have
been carried out. To keep the processing of receiver simple,
the RSS-based crowd-sensing schemes are designed for MSL,
which require the receivers to report their perceived RSS
values [26], [27]. Nevertheless, their application is limited to
very speciﬁc cases where the source devices are separated
with a signiﬁcant distance of tens or hundreds of meters
and all the radio channel characteristics as well as the
transmit power values are known at the receiver. Furthermore,
some MSL approaches estimate the location of each source
separately, depending on the distinguishability of signals
[28], [29]. Nevertheless, signals from different source devices
are intractable to separate, especially in noncooperative and
spectrum sharing situations. With the AOA estimation results,
a classiﬁcation process is proposed to associate each estimate
with the corresponding source, which is modeled as an
optimization problem and solved by the brute force search
in the sense of least square error [30]. However, the com-
putational complexity dramatically increases as the number
of sources increases. To solve this problem, by taking into
account the statistical distribution of the AOA estimates at the
receivers, a classiﬁcation procedure is formulated using the
essentials of an evolutionary process, and optimized in the
paradigm of genetic algorithm (GA) [31].
Moreover, DL is still effective to capture the propagation
features and achieve the MSL without signal separation.
DeepTxFinder uses the RSS as the input of the convo-
lutional neural network (CNN) to regress the coordinates
directly, which always suffers from overﬁtting due to lots
of connections in fully connected layers, thus hampering
its generalization ability [32]. HMTLNet aims to formulate
the MSL as a heatmap regression problem based on fully
convolutional network, learning a nonlinear mapping func-
tion from the input RSS measurements to a heatmap [33].
DeepMTL solves the MSL problem with CNN from the view
of computer vision, while it relies on the dense deployment of
receivers due to the assumption of the corwdsourced sensing
architecture [34]. It is worth mentioning that the regression-
based or image-based schemes require hundreds of receivers
and have to predict the continuous location variables in the
solution space [35]. To lower the difﬁculty of regressive
mapping, authors in [36] utilize the classiﬁcation to narrow
the solution space by dividing the continuous study area into
grids at ﬁrst, and then a multitask gated CNN (MT-GCNN)
is proposed to decompose the MSL problem into a coarse
localization task and a ﬁne correction task [36]. In addition,
two computationally light methods are developed in [7] to
handle the measurement-source association problem, where
both RSS and AOA measurements are required to obtain.
In summary, although the existing methods can solve the
problem of MSL for overlapped signals to a certain extent,
there are still the following problems.
1) There are restrictions on the distribution of source
devices, requiring that the distribution of source devices
is far away.
2) It is necessary to combine multitype measurements to
acquire more information and solve it through traditional
optimization methods, resulting in high requirements on
receivers in terms of obtaining multitype measurements.
For example, RSS-based methods require to know the
channel information as well as the transmit power, while
TOA-based and TDOA-based methods require the high
precision time synchronization.
3) It does not analyze the overlapping situation of the
received overlapped signals, but starts from the whole
received signal directly, which inevitably encounters the
problem of measurement-source association.
4) DL-based methods involve a large number of ofﬂine
training processes, which need to collect a large amount
of data, bringing challenges to practical applications.
To address these challenges, we ﬁrst analyze the characteristics
of the received signal by means of time-frequency analysis,
and design the signal extraction algorithm on this basis to
extract the pure part of the signal corresponding to each source
device to convert the problem of MSL into SSL, avoiding
the problem of measurement-source association. Then, con-
sidering that the AOA-based methods are highly desirable in
many practical applications due to the fact that they generally
work in a noncooperative, stealthy and passive manner without
requiring the prior knowledge of channel parameters and
the internode synchronization, the AOA-based triangulation
method is utilized in this article to realize localization.
Notably, 2-D time-frequency spectrogram detection (TFSD)
has received extensive attention and made great progress
in broadband spectrum sensing, which can obtain the time-
frequency information (TFI) of each time-frequency block
(TFB) well. While when the TFB is the overlapped results
of multiple signal components, the existing TFSD methods
can not known the speciﬁc TFI of each signal component
contained in it but only the contour TFI of the whole TFB.
This makes it impossible to obtain the nonoverlapping part of
each signal only by means of time-domain interception and
frequency-domain ﬁltering, that is, the problem is still dealing
with overlapped signals that contain multiple components. In
this way, when estimate the AOA of the received overlapped
signals at each receiver, we can not match the AOA estimates
to the corresponding signal component, which is deﬁned as
the index ambiguity in this article.
In general, the intention of the designed scheme is to elim-
inate the index ambiguity with the designed signal processing
algorithm after obtaining the TFSD results, i.e., obtain the TFI
of each signal component and acquire the AOA information
of each signal component associated to multiple receivers to

III. INDEX AMBIGUITY ELIMINATION-BASED
MSL FOR OVERLAPPED SIGNALS
As mentioned above, the AOA information of each signal
component can not be extracted directly, which means that
the pairing problem between the source devices and the
AOA estimates of multiple receivers can not be avoided. In
view of this, the index ambiguity elimination scheme for
overlapped signals is designed with the assistance of TFSD,
i.e, determination of the detailed TFI and the AOA information
of each signal component contained in the TFB from the
overlapped signals. Then, the location of each source device
can be estimated by combining the AOAs acquired by multiple
receivers.
Speciﬁcally, the designed index ambiguity elimination
scheme is shown in Fig. 2(c), where the processing procedure
consists of three main parts, extraction of nonoverlapping part
of TFBs, signal segmentation of the extracted nonoverlapping
TFB, and acquisition of AOA information.
A. Analysis of Overlapping Types Inter-TFB
In order to study the extraction of nonoverlapping part
of TFBs, we ﬁrst analyze and classify the possible overlap-
ping types of the received overlapped signals at the TFB
level (inter-TFB), as shown in Fig. 3. Speciﬁcally, it can
be seen that there are seven different types of overlapped
signals, i.e., Types (a)–(g). Fig. 3(a) shows the time-frequency
spectrograms of seven different overlapping types. Fig. 3(b)
shows the TFSD results of seven different overlapping types,
where the black rectangle box represents the TFSD result of
each TFB. Fig. 3(c) shows the results of nonoverlapping part
extraction for time-frequency spectrograms of seven different
overlapping types, where the red rectangle box represents the
possible extraction results for TFB-1 and the blue rectangle
box represents the possible extraction results for TFB-2.
After the analysis of the most advanced TFSD meth-
ods [39], [40], [41], we learn that:
1) For Type (a), (b), and (c), the TFSD methods can usually
acquire the TFI for each TFB.
2) For Type (d), the TFSD methods can obtain the TFI for
TFB-1, while for TFB-2, there may be two results, i.e.,
the solid line black rectangle box and the dotted line
black rectangle box.
3) For Type (e), the TFSD methods can only obtain the
contour TFI for the whole TFB, but not the TFB
corresponding to each device.
4) For Type (f), when the RSS of TFB-1 is greater than
that of TFB-2, only the contour TFI for the whole TFB
can be recognized; if the RSS of TFB-2 is greater than
that of TFB-1, the TFI for TFB-1 and TFB-2 can be
obtained, respectively.
5) For Type (g), only the TFI for the whole TFB can be
obtained.
In general, for Types (a)–(f), we can obtain the nonover-
lapping parts of TFB-1 or TFB-2 by designing appropriate
signal processing algorithm; while for Type (g), we can only
analyze the whole overlapped signal. In addition, it is worth
mentioning that two independent source devices can hardly
have the same frequency-domain information [flower, fupper]
and time-domain information [tstart, tend] in practice. That
is, even if two devices have the same frequency-domain
information, in most cases they do not completely overlap in
the time-domain, such as Type (e).

C. Analysis of Overlapping Types Intra-TFB
Up to now, we can eliminate the overlaps inter-TFB, but
whether the extracted nonoverlapping TFB itself (intra-TFB)
contains only the signal of a single source device needs further
analysis.
In view of this, we analyze the possible compositions of the
extracted TFB, as shown in Fig. 4. It can be observed that there
are four types, Type (1) means only one signal component in
the extracted TFB, and Types (2)–(4) mean multiple signal
components in the extracted TFB. Nevertheless, during the
actual processing procedure, we can not obtain this speciﬁc
composition information of the extracted TFB in advance, so
it is necessary to further analyze the extracted TFB before
extracting the AOA information.

In the ﬁeld of rank estimation, many excellent algorithms
have emerged in the past few decades, including information
theoretic criterion (such as Akaike information criterion (AIC)
[42] and minimum description length (MDL) criterion [42]),
hypothesis testing (such as Wishart-matrix’s largest eigenvalue
(WME) [43]), and Gerschgorin disk estimator (GDE) [44].
Among them, AIC has better estimation performance at low
SNR region, but it is not a consistent estimation. MDL is a
consistent estimator, but its performance is worse than AIC at
low SNR region. The main idea of WME is starting from the
perspective of the distribution characteristics of the detection
statistics constructed by the eigenvalues of the received signal
covariance matrix to improve the rank estimation performance
in large sample region. GDE utilizes the orthogonality of

A. Preparations
1) Simulation Parameter Settings: There are Q Ds sharing
the current frequency band to conduct their own data trans-
mission. P = 3 Rs equipped with UCA that M = 12 are used
to receive the signals and conduct localization. The carrier
frequency fc is set to 5.8 GHz, the sampling rate is 16 MHz,
the sample time is 3.4 ms, and the length of the received
signal samples is L = 54400. For the STFT, the number of
DFT points is N = 256, then the dimension of spectrogram
X is 256 × 212. During the process of the sliding window
based feature extraction, the choice of the window length ϱ is
related to the rank estimation performance and the processing
speed, while the choice of the step length ς is related to the
subsequent segmentation accuracy and the processing speed.
Therefore, by considering the two factors of performance and
processing speed, we choose the window length of ϱ = 500
and step length of ς = 100.

localization performance, we change the RSS by ﬁxing the

the location of each D can be expressed as (d cos(θ), d sin(θ)).
Meanwhile, to reﬂect the impact of the difference in RSS
of different Ds to R on the localization performance, the
parameter η = 2 is used to make the difference between
different Ds’ transmit power. For example, when the transmit
power is set to 5 dBm, the transmit power of each D will be
uniformly distributed on [5 −(η/2), 5 + (η/2)] dBm.

spectrum sensing scenario, the designed scheme can obtain
the time-frequency resource usage information of each source
while completing the MSL, which can provide more useful
information than the traditional GA-based localization method.

VI. CONCLUSION
In this article, we proposed an index ambiguity elimination-
based MSL scheme, which provides a new way to realize MSL
for overlapped signals. Notably, the designed scheme does not
require the combination of multitype measurements reducing
the requirement on the receiver, and avoids the problem of
measurement-source association. Speciﬁcally, we analyzed and
summarized the overlapping types at TFB level and signal
level, and designed the TFB nonoverlapping part extraction
algorithm and signal segmentation algorithm to extract the
pure part of each signal component for AOA estimation, and
then realized the localization through AOA-based triangulation
method. In addition, the effectiveness and feasibility of the
designed MSL scheme in the actual environment were veriﬁed
by building an experiment system. Nevertheless, we only
assume that the source devices are all single antenna at present,
that is, the rank estimation result is equal to the number
of source devices. While with the widespread application
of multiantenna technology, source devices are likely to be
equipped with multiple antennas for wireless communication.
The utilization of multiple antennas brings new challenges
to the estimation of the number of devices, which is not
satisﬁed that the rank estimation result is equal to the number
of devices. In the future, it is necessary to extend the study
scenario to the case of multiple antennas.
