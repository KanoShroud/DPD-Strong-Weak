1. Introduction

   Planar microphone array-based beamforming is a popular technology for acoustic source identification from medium to long
measurement distances. It has been widely used in automobiles, airplanes, high-speed trains, wind turbines and other fields [1–6]. The
classical Delay and Sum (DAS) beamforming suffers from poor spatial resolution at low frequencies and many spurious ghost sources at
high frequencies [7]. Thus, the acoustic imaging is not clear, and the source identification performance is poor. To improve the
performance of DAS, many well-known deconvolution methods have been proposed, such as deconvolution approach for the mapping
of  acoustic  sources  (DAMAS)  [8],  non-negative  least-squares  (NNLS)  [9],  Richardson-Lucy  (RL)  [9],  fast  iterative
shrinkage-thresholding algorithm (FISTA) [10] and their FFT-based variants [8–12], CLEAN [13], CLEAN based on source coherence
(CLEAN-SC) [13] and its high-resolution versions [14–16]. Ref. [17] proposed a global optimization method to search for the position
and strength of the source by maximizing the agreement between model and measurement. This method has good source identification
