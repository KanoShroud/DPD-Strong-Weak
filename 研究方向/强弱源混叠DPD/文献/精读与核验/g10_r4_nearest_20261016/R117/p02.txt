Two-dimensional (2D) compressive beamforming utilizing planar microphone arrays and compressive sensing theory [18,19] has
attracted much attention due to its wide source identification space, applicability to both coherent and incoherent sources, and clear
imaging [20–24]. Conventional compressive beamforming discretizes the target source area, establishes underdetermined equations
between the unknown source strength distribution and the measured sound pressure of array microphones, and solves them to obtain
the coordinate and strength estimates of the sources. However, it suffers from the basis mismatch issue [25] and performance
degradation under small source spacings (much less than the Rayleigh criterion distance) or low signal to noise ratios (SNRs) [21,26].
The basis mismatch issue is that when the source lies off the discretized grid point, the performance of compressive beamforming
degrades. Dense grids can only alleviate this problem to a certain extent, but at the expense of increased computational cost. Moreover,
the over-dense grids lead to excessive coherence of the sensing matrix columns (steering vectors), which causes algebraic aliasing and
deteriorates the source identification performance [26,27]. Besides, the poor source identification performance under small source
spacings or low SNRs is also related to the coherence of the sensing matrix columns.
   To solve the basis mismatch issue, many scholars have made efforts and proposed three types of improved compressive beam­
forming methods: grid-free/gridless methods [24,28-30], dynamic grid methods [31–33], and off-grid methods [23,34-36]. The
grid-free method deals the target source area as a continuum, establishes the atomic norm minimization model of microphone sound
pressure induced by the acoustic sources, and solves it to obtain the source distribution. It can fundamentally circumvent basis
mismatch but is only compatible with rectangular arrays with uniformly distributed microphones and sparse rectangular arrays. The
dynamic grid method constructs an objective function with the grid coordinates and the source strength distribution as variables,
establishes its replacement function utilizing an optimized minimization framework to reduce the computational complexity, and
iteratively solves it by the gradient descent method to make the initial grid coordinates and source strength distribution gradually
approximate the true values. It is compatible with arbitrary geometry planar array, but its computational efficiency is low, and its
performance and computational efficiency are affected by the iteration step size. Off-grid methods mainly include the off-grid
compressive beamforming that utilizes Taylor expansion to compensate for off-grid deviations [23] and Newtonized orthogonal
matching pursuit (NOMP) [37] compressive beamforming that uses Newtonian optimization to search for the true acoustic source
position [36]. The former regards the grid-based DOA estimates and the 2D off-grid DOA compensations as blocks to establish a
block-sparse [38] off-grid compressive beamforming model and solves it to obtain the source coordinate estimates. The latter es­
tablishes a maximum likelihood estimate with the source coordinates and source strengths as parameters, and utilizes on-grid initial
estimate and subsequent Newtonian optimization to obtain the accurate source coordinates and source strengths estimate. It is
compatible with arbitrary geometry planar array, has high computational efficiency, and does not require the prior knowledge of SNR.
Besides, it enjoys a high spatial resolution due to the cyclic feedback Newtonian optimization.
   To improve the sound source identification performance under small source spacings or low SNRs, many scholars have tried multi-
snapshot compressive beamforming [26,36,39-43]. Based on a time-invariant signal assumption, the multi-snapshot methods process
synchronously multiple data blocks (snapshots) of the array signal that share the same source sparsity by joint sparse recovery al­
gorithm to obtain the source strength distribution. The joint processing can promote spatio-temporal common sparsity and enhance the
anti-interference ability. The increased rank of microphone sound pressure matrix brings improved spatial resolution and source
localization and quantification accuracy. Multi-snapshot versions of conventional grid-based compressive beamforming, grid-free
compressive beamforming, and off-grid compressive beamforming have been developed. The authors in this paper also developed a
2D multi-snapshot off-grid compressive beamforming based on NOMP. It enhanced anti-interference ability and had better identifi­
cation performance for closely-spaced sources than the single-snapshot version [36]. However, limited by the time-invariant signal
assumption [26], multi-snapshot methods are only applicable to stationary sources. Recently, Park et al. [44] and Ang et al. [45]
proposed the multi-frequency synchronous methods with similar ideas and performance to the multi-snapshot methods. These
multi-frequency methods do not require the time-invariant signal assumption and are applicable to both stationary and nonstationary
acoustic sources. The method of Park et al. is grid-based and compatible with arbitrary geometry planar array, but it cannot overcome
the basis mismatch issue. The method of Ang et al. is grid-free, which can solve basis mismatch issue, but it is only suitable for
one-dimensional uniform linear arrays and can only identify sources in a locally limited area in front of and co-planar with the array.
Hitherto, no method can solve the two aforementioned problems (the basis mismatch problem and the performance degradation under
small sources spacings or low SNRs) while being compatible with arbitrary geometry planar arrays and both stationary and nonsta­
tionary acoustic sources.
   In this paper, we proposed a multi-frequency synchronous 2D off-grid compressive beamforming. This method first formulates the
acoustic source identification in a 2D continuous plane as a maximum likelihood estimation model with source coordinates and the
source strengths of all frequencies as parameters. Then, a block-NOMP algorithm (BNOMP) that processes multiple frequencies
synchronously is developed to solve the model. The proposed method can overcome the basis mismatch issue, improve the identifi­
cation performance under small source spacings or low SNRs, and it is compatible with arbitrary geometry planar arrays and applicable
to both stationary and nonstationary acoustic sources.

microphone arrays. Section 3 and Section 4 establish the multi-frequency synchronous on-grid and off-grid compressive beamforming,
respectively. The former is the basis for the latter. Section 5 demonstrates the performance of the proposed method through the
identification simulations for stationary and impulsive acoustic sources. Section 6 discusses the experiments performed and Section 7
gathers the conclusions.
