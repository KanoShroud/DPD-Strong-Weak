   To further clarify the acoustic source identification performance of the proposed BNOMP-CB, we compare it with the above-
mentioned state-of-the-art methods (FB, DAMAS and CLEAN-SC) based on multiple-snapshot measurement of the first simulation
case. The three sources are set to be incoherent with each other, and noise is added with a SNR of 20 dB. In this simulation, the number
of snapshots is 50 (When the number of snapshots is insufficient, the acoustic source identification performance of the above methods
will be significantly degraded. When using a single snapshot data, which is equivalent to the case of coherent sources, FB and CLEAN-
SC will even fail [7, 50].), the exponent parameter of FB is set to 30, and each frequency is processed separately. Since FB and
CLEAN-SC need a complete microphone sound pressure cross-spectrum matrix (CSM), we have performed diagonal reconstruction
(DR) [51] on the CSM for denoising. We also use the CSM with DR for DAMAS, although the CSM with diagonal removal [7, 52] is also
suitable for DAMAS, and both have comparable effects. Other settings are the same as the above simulation. Fig. 3 shows the colormaps
of the three methods, which superimposes the energies of identified results under different frequencies. The true locations are marked
by the symbol “+”. The estimated source strengths are marked near the identified sources (corresponding to the peaks of hotspots) in
the colormaps. For FB and CLEAN-SC, the peak value of hotspots in the colormaps is directly considered as the strength estimate, while
for DAMAS, the integral energy of the grid points in hotspots is considered as the strength estimate. It can be seen that for the
closely-spaced sources S1 and S2, all three methods cannot separate them correctly under the four frequencies. The mainlobe is heavily
fused, and the source is located in the middle of the two sources. In short, FB, DAMAS and CLEAN-SC all failed to identify them. The
three methods can accurately locate and quantify the source S3 with only 3000 Hz frequency components, but the mainlobe of FB is
wide. Comparing Fig. 2 and Fig. 3, it can be seen that the spatial resolution of the proposed BNOMP-CB is higher than that of the above
three methods.
   To illustrate the anti-interference performance of the proposed method, we designed a case in which three sources are far apart and
lie off the grid points. The coordinates of the three acoustic sources S1, S2, and S3 are ( −0.18, −0.2), (0.08, −0.2), and (0.3, 0.36) m,
respectively, and all the three sources contain components with frequencies of 2000, 3000 and 4000 Hz. The source strength amplitude
vectors of S1 and S2 are both [0, 1, 1, 1, 0] Pa, while that of S3 is [0, 1, 1, 0.4, 0] Pa. Fig. 4 shows the source identification results of the
four methods with SNR of 20 dB. Fig. 5 shows the source identification results with SNR of 5 dB. As Fig. 4, all the four methods can
correctly locate all the sources with SNR of 20 dB. The on-grid OMP-CB (Fig. 4(a) and (c)) and BOMP-CB (Fig. 4(e)), affected by basis
mismatch, locate the sources to the grid points near the true sources. Compared to the on-grid methods, the off-grid NOMP-CB and
BNOMP-CB have significantly improved accuracy in source localization and strength quantification. Comparing Fig. 4(a)-(d) with
Fig. 5(a)-(d), we can see that the identification results of the single-frequency OMP-CB and NOMP-CB are more scattered when the SNR
is 5 dB than when the SNR is 20 dB. Especially for the weak source, with a strength of 0.4 Pa at 4000 Hz, both single-frequency
processing methods result in a large deviation in localization due to noise interference. In contrast, with SNR decreases from 20 dB
to 5 dB, the multi-frequency synchronous BOMP-CB and BNOMP-CB only have a slight decrease in localization accuracy. According to
the quantification for each frequency component of the sources, we can see that whether SNR is 20 dB or 5 dB, BNOMP-CB has the
highest source strength estimation accuracy and outputs the lowest strength estimation at the frequencies that true sources do not
involve. In summary, the proposed BNOMP-CB has the strongest capability to resist noise interference than the other three methods.
