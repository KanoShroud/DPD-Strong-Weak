    Fig. 10(a) shows that when SNR is greater than 20 dB, the localization errors of OMP-CB and BOMP-CB are about 0.074 m and
0.022 m, NOMP-CB is about 0.018 m, and BNOMP-CB is only about 0.001 m. It can be seen that in terms of localization accuracy under
high SNR, the off-grid methods are superior to the grid-based methods, due to their ability to overcome the basis mismatch, and
BNOMP-CB is the highest. With the decrease of SNR, the localization errors of OMP-CB and NOMP-CB increase significantly, while the
localization errors of BOMP-CB and BNOMP-CB are stable and only slightly increase at 0 dB. When SNR is lower than 15 dB, the
localization error of NOMP-CB is higher than that of BOMP-CB and BNOMP-CB, that is to say, the multi-frequency synchronous
processing methods show better source identification performance at low SNR. In summary, the acoustic source identification per­
formance of the single-frequency methods is greatly affected by interference, while the multi-frequency synchronous processing
significantly enhances the ability to resist interference. A similar conclusion can be drawn from the quantification error curve shown in
Fig. 10(b). As can be seen from Fig. 10(c) the law of narrowband acoustic sources identification is generally consistent with that of the
multiple-sinusoids acoustic sources identification. In other words, the off-grid NOMP-CB and BNOMP-CB show better performance at
high SNR, while BOMP-CB and BNOMP-CB with multi-frequency synchronous processing perform better at low SNR. However,
regardless of SNR, BNOMP-CB always enjoys the best acoustic source identification performance.
   Comparing the results of the two Monte Carlo data sets, we can find that the localization errors of OMP-CB and NOMP-CB cor­
responding to the first data set are significantly greater than those corresponding to the second data set, while the localization errors of
BOMP-CB and BNOMP-CB have little difference between the two data sets. This is because the first data set contains many low-
frequency components, while the second data set does not. The acoustic source identification performance of single-frequency
methods is strongly frequency-dependent, presenting low localization accuracy and poor spatial resolution at low frequencies. Due
to the introduction of multi-frequency synchronous processing, both BOMP-CB and BNOMP-CB can achieve enhanced performance,
allowing them to obtain excellent results for both data sets.
