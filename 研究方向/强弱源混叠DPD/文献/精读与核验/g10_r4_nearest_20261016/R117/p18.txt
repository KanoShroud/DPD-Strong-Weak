6. Experiments

  An identification experiment of 3 small loudspeaker sources (Abramtek Type M5, Shenzhen, China) excited by random noise was
performed outdoors. The array and acoustic source distribution are shown in Fig. 11, where the planar microphone array is a Brüel &
Kjær (Type 8608, Nærum, Denmark) 36-channel sector wheel array with a diameter of 0.65 m and an average microphone spacing of
0.1 m. The three loudspeakers are distributed in a plane parallel to the array and 1 m away from it. In this experiment, a PULSE data
acquisition and analysis system (Brüel & Kjær Type 3660C, Nærum, Denmark) was used to synchronously sample the sound pressure
signals of 36 microphones. The collected microphone sound pressure signals were performed FFT on BK Connect™ (Brüel & Kjær Type
8404, Nærum, Denmark) to obtain Fourier spectra with a frequency resolution of 50 Hz. We extract the components of Fourier spectra
under 3 different frequency combinations of [1000, 1050, …, 5000] , [1000, 2000, …, 5000] , and [3000, 3050, …, 3200] Hz and use
the aforementioned four methods to process them to obtain the acoustic source imaging results.
    Fig. 12 is the acoustic source localization colormaps under a wideband of [1000, 1050, …, 5000] Hz (L = 81). Fig. 12(a) and (b)
show that in the entire frequency band, the localization results of OMP-CB are scattered, while the localization results of NOMP-CB are
more concentrated. However, at many frequencies, the localization accuracy of these two methods is low, and there are many spurious
sources far from the true source. In contrast, both BOMP-CB and BNOMP-CB can locate these acoustic sources correctly. For the left two
sources close to each other (S1 and S2), at many frequencies, OMP-CB and NOMP-CB fail to separate them, while BOMP-CB and
BNOMP-CB both succeed in separating them and obtain accurate coordinate estimates. Besides, comparing the localization results of
BOMP-CB and BNOMP-CB for the source S2 and S3, we can see that BNOMP-CB can overcome the basis mismatch problem through
Newtonian optimization and obtain better localization than BOMP-CB.
    Fig. 13 is the localization colormaps with multiple harmonic frequencies of [1000, 2000, …, 5000] Hz (L = 5) to demonstrate the
performance of the proposed method under multiple intermittent frequencies. It can be seen from this figure that OMP-CB can
accurately locate the source S1. However, since the strength of the source S2 is weak at the frequencies of interest, OMP-CB locates this
source scatted. Compared to OMP-CB, NOMP-CB improves acoustic source identification accuracy, which is attributed to the intro­
duction of Newtonian optimization. In contrast, both BOMP-CB and BNOMP-CB obtain accurate coordinate estimates, and BNOMP-CB
is superior to BOMP-CB. These phenomena are similar to those in processing with a wideband of [1000, 1050, …, 5000] Hz. This case
demonstrates that the proposed method enjoys good source identification performance, even with only a few processing frequencies.
    Fig. 14 is the localization colormaps with a narrowband of [3000, 3050, …, 3200] Hz (L = 5) to demonstrate the performance of the
proposed method in identifying narrowband sources. As shown in this figure, OMP-CB cannot locate the two left sources (S1 and S2)
correctly, and their localization results are blurred and fused with each other. For the source S3, due to the basis mismatch, OMP-CB
locates it to the grid points near the actual position, which causes the energy of different frequencies to be dispersed. NOMP-CB
successfully separates the left two sources (S1 and S2) with small spacing at all 5 frequencies and obtains their correct coordinates
estimates, which is significantly improved compared to OMP-CB. However, the localization results under different frequencies are still
not completely consistent. The multi-frequency synchronous BOMP-CB and BNOMP-CB can locate all the sources with an accuracy
comparable to the above two frequency conditions.
   In addition, for the above case of 5 frequencies, four methods consume about 0.005, 0.248, 0.205, and 0.539 s on a 3.5 GHz Intel(R)
Xeon(R) E5–1650 v3 CPU (only for solving), respectively. For the above case of 81 frequencies, four methods consume about 0.034,
5.794, 195.748, and 240.108 s, respectively. It can be seen that as the number of processing frequencies increases, the time consumed
by BOMP-CB and BNOMP-CB with multi-frequency simultaneous processing increases sharply due to the increase in the dimension of
the sensing matrix. Reducing the number of processing frequencies helps improve the efficiency of the proposed method.
