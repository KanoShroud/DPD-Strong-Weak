    Fig. 15 shows the acoustic source localization colormaps and source strength quantification curves under a wideband of
[1000, 1050, …, 5000] Hz (L = 81). It can be seen from Fig. 15(e) and (f) that at low frequencies, both OMP-CB and NOMP-CB show
large quantification deviations, which corresponds to the failed localization (as shown in Fig. 15(a) and (b)). In comparison, NOMP-CB
can obtain accurate quantification at lower frequencies than OMP-CB, which is attributed to the higher spatial resolution of NOMP-CB
than OMP-CB. Both BOMP-CB and BNOMP-CB have almost constant quantification deviations at all frequencies due to synchronous
processing (as shown in Fig. 15(e) and (f)). Consistent with the lower localization deviation of BNOMP-CB than BOMP-CB (as shown in
Fig. 15(c) and (d)), the quantitative deviation of BNOMP-CB is also lower than that of BOMP-CB, which is attributed to the overcoming
of the basis mismatch. In addition, as we can see from Fig. 15(g), for the source S3, all 4 methods can obtain accurate quantification
results over the entire frequency band. In summary, BNOMP-CB enjoys the best quantification performance, BOMP-CB ranks the
second, NOMP-CB the third, and OMP-CB the worst.
    Fig. 16 is the acoustic source localization colormaps and source strength quantification results with multiple harmonic frequencies
of [1000, 2000, …, 5000] Hz (L = 5). It can be seen that for the sources S1 and S2, OMP-CB cannot correctly separate them at 3000 Hz
and below, and the quantification is also inaccurate. NOMP-CB cannot separate the sources S1 and S2 at 2000 Hz and below. It again
proves the higher spatial resolution of NOMP-CB than OMP-CB. BOMP-CB cannot accurately locate and quantify the two sources due to
the low spatial resolution of low-frequency components and the basis mismatch. BNOMP-CB successfully locates the two sources and
accurately quantifies their source strength amplitude of all frequencies. Similarly, for the source S3, all 4 methods obtain accurate
quantification results at 5 frequencies. This case again proves that the proposed method enjoys good source identification performance,
even with only a few processing frequencies.
