High Resolution Direct Position Determination
of Radio Frequency Sources
Tom Tirer and Anthony J. Weiss
Abstract—The most common methods for localization of radio
frequency transmitters are based on two processing steps. In the
ﬁrst step, parameters such as angle of arrival or time of arrival are
estimated at each base station independently. In the second step,
the estimated parameters are used to determine the location of
the transmitters. The direct position determination approach ad-
vocates using the observations from all the base stations together
in order to estimate the locations in a single step. This single-step
method is known to outperform two-step methods when the signal-
to-noise ratio is low. In this paper, we propose a direct-position-de-
termination-based method for localization of multiple emitters that
transmit unknown signals. The method does not require knowledge
of the number of emitters. It is based on minimum-variance-dis-
tortionless-response considerations to achieve a high resolution es-
timator that requires only a two-dimensional search for planar ge-
ometry, and a three-dimensional search for the general case.
Index Terms—Array processing, direct position determination,
emitter localization, maximum likelihood, minimum variance
distortionless response (MVDR), multiple signal classiﬁcation
(MUSIC).
I. INTRODUCTION
T
HE problem of emitter location attracts much interest
in different disciplines such as signal processing, com-
munications, vehicular technology, and underwater acoustics.
Common localization methods use two processing steps. In the
ﬁrst step, intermediate parameters that depend on the locations
of the sources are estimated from the received signal. These
parameters are usually the angle of arrival (AOA), time of
arrival (TOA), Doppler frequency shift or signal strength.
In the second step, the previously estimated parameters are
used to estimate the locations of the sources, using geometric
considerations. From estimation theory point of view, these
methods are sub-optimal, since the parameters estimation in the
ﬁrst phase is done independently by each sensor, without using
the constraint that all the observations relate to the same emitter
location. The Direct Position Determination (DPD) approach
was proposed in [1] for a single emitter, and in [2] for multiple
emitters whose number is known. The DPD takes advantage of

the rather simple channel that is associated with radio frequency
signals, uses the observations from all the sensors together, and
ends with a cost function that depends only on the location of
sources. Therefore, it enables direct estimation of the locations
in a single step. The DPD inherently overcomes the problem of
associating estimated parameters with their relevant sources,
and was shown to outperform two-step methods, especially in
low signal to noise ratio (SNR) scenarios. Furthermore, it can
localize more simultaneous emitters than the two-step methods,
since it uses the observations from all the sensors together.
The DPD technique requires the transmission of the received
signals (possibly sampled) to a central processing location,
unlike two-step methods, that require only the transmission
of the intermediate parameters. Therefore, the DPD requires
higher communication bandwidth. A DPD estimator can be
based on the well-known Maximum Likelihood (ML) estimator
[1]. It can also be obtained by a spectral based method [2], in
order to make it computationally attractive for multiple emitters
scenario.
Spectral methods of AOA estimation can be modiﬁed to
handle direct location determination. A common spectral
method of AOA estimation for multiple sources is the Mul-
tiple Signal Classiﬁcation (MUSIC) [3] that makes use of
the eigen-structure of the measurements covariance matrix.
However, the number of sources must be known or estimated.
Furthermore, it usually requires large number of snapshots, and
coherent signals require appropriate adjustments. Other spectral
methods include the simple beamformer, which is basically the
ML estimator for a single source, and the Minimum Variance
Distortionless Response (MVDR) estimator [4]. The MVDR is
known to yield better resolution than the beamformer.
Our proposed method combines the DPD concept and the
MVDR approach in order to create a high resolution estimator
that can localize multiple dense emitters that transmit unknown
signals. Since prior knowledge of the number of signals is not
required, the use of model order determination [5] is avoided.
Recall that order determination is prone to errors when the
number of snapshots is small, the SNR is low or the sources
are dense. The proposed method generates a spectrum (heat
map) over a plane, in the 2D case, or over a three dimensional
space in the general case, with peaks at points where emitters
are likely to be located. Determining the number of emitters
(and their locations) can be performed as a ﬁnal step using
Constant-False-Alarm-Rate (CFAR) methods inspired by radar
and sonar literature [6].
Direct localization based on MVDR and AOA arrays was
suggested in [7] and [8]. The work reported in [7] discusses un-
synchronized arrays, therefore it does not exploit TOA or differ-
ential TOA, and the arrays must consist of two sensors or more.

V. CONCLUSIONS
We proposed a technique that combines the direct position
determination and the MVDR approaches and produces a high
resolution spectrum for multiple sources localization. The pro-
posed estimator can be implemented without knowing or esti-
mating the number of sources. It can work even with spatially
