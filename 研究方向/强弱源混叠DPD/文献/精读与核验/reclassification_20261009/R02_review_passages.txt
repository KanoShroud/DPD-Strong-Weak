[PDF p.1] Abstract—The most common methods for localization of radio frequency transmitters are based on two processing steps. In the ﬁrst step, parameters such as angle of arrival or time of arrival are estimated at each base station independently. In the second step, the estimated parameters are used to determine the location of the transmitters. The direct position determination approach ad- vocates using the observations from all the base stations together in order to estimate the locations in a single step. This single-step method is known to outperform two-step methods when the signal- to-noise ratio is low. In this paper, we propose a direct-position-de- termination-based method for localization of multiple emitters that transmit unknown signals. The method does not require knowledge of the number of emitters. It is based on minimum-variance-dis- tortionless-response considerations to achieve a high resolution es- timator that requires only a two-dimensional search for planar ge- ometry, and a three-dimensional search for the general case.

[PDF p.1] HE problem of emitter location attracts much interest in different disciplines such as signal processing, com- munications, vehicular technology, and underwater acoustics. Common localization methods use two processing steps. In the ﬁrst step, intermediate parameters that depend on the locations of the sources are estimated from the received signal. These parameters are usually the angle of arrival (AOA), time of arrival (TOA), Doppler frequency shift or signal strength. In the second step, the previously estimated parameters are used to estimate the locations of the sources, using geometric considerations. From estimation theory point of view, these methods are sub-optimal, since the parameters estimation in the ﬁrst phase is done independently by each sensor, without using the constraint that all the observations relate to the same emitter location. The Direct Position Determination (DPD) approach was proposed in [1] for a single emitter, and in [2] for multiple emitters whose number is known. The DPD takes advantage of

[PDF p.1] the rather simple channel that is associated with radio frequency signals, uses the observations from all the sensors together, and ends with a cost function that depends only on the location of sources. Therefore, it enables direct estimation of the locations in a single step. The DPD inherently overcomes the problem of associating estimated parameters with their relevant sources, and was shown to outperform two-step methods, especially in low signal to noise ratio (SNR) scenarios. Furthermore, it can localize more simultaneous emitters than the two-step methods, since it uses the observations from all the sensors together. The DPD technique requires the transmission of the received signals (possibly sampled) to a central processing location, unlike two-step methods, that require only the transmission of the intermediate parameters. Therefore, the DPD requires higher communication bandwidth. A DPD estimator can be based on the well-known Maximum Likelihood (ML) estimator [1]. It can also be obtained by a spectral based method [2], in order to make it computationally attractive for multiple emitters scenario.

[PDF p.1] Our proposed method combines the DPD concept and the MVDR approach in order to create a high resolution estimator that can localize multiple dense emitters that transmit unknown signals. Since prior knowledge of the number of signals is not required, the use of model order determination [5] is avoided. Recall that order determination is prone to errors when the number of snapshots is small, the SNR is low or the sources are dense. The proposed method generates a spectrum (heat map) over a plane, in the 2D case, or over a three dimensional space in the general case, with peaks at points where emitters are likely to be located. Determining the number of emitters (and their locations) can be performed as a ﬁnal step using Constant-False-Alarm-Rate (CFAR) methods inspired by radar and sonar literature [6].
