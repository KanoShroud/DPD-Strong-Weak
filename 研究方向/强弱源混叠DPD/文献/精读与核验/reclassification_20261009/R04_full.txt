TDOA Based Direct Positioning of Co-Channel Signals Using Cyclic
Cross-Spectral Functions
Nan Xia , Runlin Xiang , Baohui Xing , and Danyang Gao
Abstract— Passive positioning of co-channel transmitters is a
major challenge in radio monitoring. Co-channel signals overlap
in both the time and frequency domains but have different cyclic
frequencies. A direct localization method of multiple co-channel
signals is proposed in this letter based on the relative time
delay information of collected signals and cyclic cross-spectral
functions. It can estimate the number and cyclic frequencies
of co-channel signals and separate co-channel signals through
spectral slicing for direct positioning. A joint spectral function is
constructed with a set of randomly generated position points,
and the position estimate for each co-channel signal can be
determined by searching for the maximum value of the func-
tion. Additionally, an iterative sampling method is proposed to
improve the positioning accuracy with only a few additional
iterations. The simulation results demonstrate the superiority of
the proposed algorithm over other joint positioning algorithms
in the presence of co-channel interference.
Index Terms— Direct localization, co-channel signals, cyclic
frequency, cyclic cross-spectral function, iterative sampling.
I. INTRODUCTION
T
IME-DIFFERENCE-OF-ARRIVAL (TDOA) localization
is an important technology in passive positioning. Due
to its low cost and high reliability, it has been widely used
in radio monitoring, wireless communications, radar, naviga-
tion and has become a research hotspot in recent years [1].
The traditional TDOA localization is based on the two-step
method. Utilizing correlation theory, parameters related to
the source location information are first estimated [2], [3].
Then, the localization method is formulated as an optimization
problem constrained by geometric relationships, and some
positioning algorithms have been proposed, such as weighted
least squares [4] and the convex relaxation method [5], which
enrich the relevant TDOA positioning theories.
The two-step positioning algorithm has good positioning
performance, but it is aimed at single-source signals. In wire-
less communications, signals sharing the same frequency is
very common. When there are multiple co-channel sources,
the target signal from the same channel is likely to be
affected by the normal communication signal or interference
signal, seriously restricting location system performance. This

Several TDOA estimation algorithms based on the two-step
method have been proposed to address the co-channel inter-
ference problem. Wan et al. [6] employed blind source
separation and secondary interference signal extraction to
reduce the impact of mixed communication signals. Ting et
al. [7] proposed an interference-suppressing method based on
particle filtering to improve TDOA estimation performance.
Liu et al. [8] applied a signal selection algorithm based on
cyclic correlation for estimating cyclostationary signal TDOAs
in the presence of interference and impulsive noise. With
the result of time delay estimation, the positioning algorithm
can be used to obtain the target location. Zhang et al. [9]
proposed a positioning method based on iterative constrained
weighted least squares, which can reduce the location devia-
tion. Ma et al. [10] used the convex difference planning tool
to perform the maximum likelihood objective function of opti-
mizing time difference positioning to ensure convergence to a
stationary point of the objective function. The above methods
are all two-step positioning methods. When interference exists,
mixing co-channel signals will lead to ambiguity in correlation
peak estimation and reduce positioning accuracy.
Some direct positioning methods have been proposed to
overcome this problem. Ouyang et al. proposed a direct
geolocation method for multiple targets [11]. This method
establishes a relationship directly between the received I/Q
data and the transmitter positions and can achieve higher
positioning accuracy under low signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs).
However, this method requires limiting the search area and
would be impractical for unknown sources localization, and
the performance of the method cannot be guaranteed when
there are insufficient location points in the search area.
Xia et al. [12] introduced a direct positioning method for
co-channel signals based on a random spatial spectrum in the
presence of multipath propagation. However, in this method,
strong signals will affect weak signals, resulting in large
positioning errors for weak signals. Li et al. [13] constructed a
parallel factor model by extracting and iteratively decompos-
ing the delay matrix, performing spectral peak search, and then
performing direct positioning based on grid search. However,
there will be overlapping spectral peaks, causing false peaks
to affect positioning. This method performs poorly in locating
weak signals and requires extensive calculations.
In this letter, to address the problem of co-channel sig-
nal interference, we propose a multitarget direct positioning
algorithm based on the relative time delay information con-
tained in the collected signals, and design a resampling method
to achieve higher estimation accuracy. The main contributions

direct positioning methods [11], [12], [13], in the presence
of co-channel interference, positioning accuracy is affected by
insufficient location points and false peaks appearing during
spectral peak searches. Other methods have larger RMSEs at
low SNRs. The proposed algorithm combines resampling and
direct localization to achieve lower positioning errors.

signals and can effectively weaken the interaction between

joint positioning error under different signal powers.

To test the impact of the number of mixed signals on

proposed method has higher positioning accuracy.
B. Outdoor Experiment
We conducted outdoor experiments to verify the practicality
of the proposed method. Five receiving stations and two
transmitting sources are set up. The distance and location
between the transmitting sources and the receiving stations are
shown in Fig. 6. We use two BPSK signals with cyclic fre-
quencies of 1 MHz and 0.8 MHz and transmit powers of 30 W
and 20 W, respectively. The center frequency is 900 MHz, the
sampling rate is 3 MHz, and the receiving device uses GPS
for time synchronization.
We conducted 50 independent experiments in real-world
scenarios and compared the RMSEs of different algorithms.
The experimental results of the different methods are shown
in Table II. The table shows that the positioning error of
the proposed method is the smallest, with the maximum and
minimum error being 11.2 m and 19.6 m, respectively. This
shows that this method is feasible in practical situations and
is better than other positioning methods.
V. CONCLUSION
A direct positioning method for co-channel transmitters
is proposed in this letter. Since the signals have different
cyclic frequencies, we first extract the spectral slices with the
estimated cyclic frequencies. Then, we use a set of weighting
cyclic spectral functions to estimate the positions of the
transmitters directly. Finally, we propose an iterative method to
refine the positioning accuracy. Simulation results show that
the proposed algorithm outperforms other joint localization
methods of co-channel signals.
