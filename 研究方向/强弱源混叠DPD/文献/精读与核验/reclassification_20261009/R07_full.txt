Abstract—To 
address 
the 
performance 
degradation caused by grid mismatch and 
unknown channel attenuation, we propose a 
time difference of arrival (TDOA)-based direct 
position determination (DPD) method with 
parametric 
dictionary 
learning. 
First, 
the 
off-grid error and the model parameter error are 
uniformly modeled as the dictionary offset 
problem. Subsequently, the DPD problem under 
dictionary perturbation is transformed into a 
joint optimization problem involving parametric 
dictionary 
learning 
and 
sparse 
signal 
reconstruction, which is solved within the sparse Bayesian learning (SBL) framework. Furthermore, the Cramér-Rao 
bounds (CRB) for TDOA-based DPD are derived, serving as a benchmark for performance evaluation. Simulation 
results demonstrate that the proposed method effectively localizes off-grid sources under unknown channel 
attenuation. The positioning accuracy can approach the CRB under high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and can be 
improved by over 20% compared with the existing methods under low SNR. 
 
Index Terms—Direct position determination (DPD), sparse Bayesian learning (SBL), parametric dictionary learning, 
off-grid error. 
 
 
I.  INTRODUCTION 
assive positioning technology is widely used in 
communications, radar, navigation, and trajectory tracking 
[1], [2]. Existing passive positioning systems predominantly 
adopt a two-step localization approach. First, positioning 
parameters are estimated from raw signals, such as direction of 
arrival (DOA) [3], time difference of arrival (TDOA) [4], and 
frequency difference of arrival (FDOA) [5]. Then, the position 
of the target is further determined based on these parameters. 
This two-step method does not require transmitting all signal 
data to a central processing station, thereby relaxing demands 
on communication bandwidth and computational load. 
However, the two-step approach also has several limitations.

First, information loss occurs at each processing stage, which 
adversely 
affects 
final 
positioning 
accuracy. 
Second, 
independent estimation at individual stations leads to the loss of 
inter-station data correlation. Finally, in multi-target scenarios, 
the two-step method faces data association challenges between 
targets and measurements. 
In response to the above challenges, Weiss and Amar 
introduced a single-step localization approach — the direct 
position determination (DPD) method [6], [7]. This method 
departs from the conventional two-step process by directly 
processing raw sampled signals and constructing a positioning 
model based on the location information embedded within the 
signals, thereby effectively mitigating the accumulation of 
errors from intermediate parameter estimation. It demonstrates 
superior performance, particularly under low SNR conditions. 
Weiss et al. primarily focused on DPD using DOA/TDOA or 
TDOA/FDOA frameworks. However, the utilization of DOA 
requires each station to be equipped with multi-element, 
multi-channel antennas, resulting in increased system 
complexity. Similarly, FDOA is only applicable when there is 
relative motion between the receiving stations and the radiation 
source. For scenarios where receiving stations employ single 
antennas and remain relatively stationary to the source, Naresh 
and Kay proposed a maximum likelihood (ML) based DPD 
method that relies solely on TDOA information [8]. This 
approach establishes a cost function for each grid point 
according to the ML criterion, with the grid point yielding the 
maximum value identified as the estimated source position. To 
address the high computational complexity associated with grid

DPD method. However, this method requires all data to be 
transmitted to a central station for processing, imposing 
significant demands on communication capabilities. Building 
upon this, Reference [10] introduced local data transmission 
between 
adjacent 
sensors, 
distributing 
computational 
complexity across individual sensors and thereby improving 
computational efficiency. It is worth noting that the methods 
mentioned above generally require a sufficiently large number 
of signal samples to achieve satisfactory positioning accuracy, 
which exhibit limitations when dealing with scenarios 
characterized by small samples. 
Compressive sensing (CS) technology demonstrates superior 
performance over traditional signal processing methods when 
dealing with limited samples. By exploiting the sparsity of 
targets in the spatial domain, many researchers have 
reformulated the 
DPD problem as a sparse signal 
reconstruction 
problem and proposed improved DPD 
algorithms, which mainly incorporates two categories: 
p
l 
norm based optimization [11], [12] and sparse Bayesian 
learning 
(SBL) 
[13], 
[14]. 
However, 
the 
successful 
reconstruction of signals using these methods hinges on the 
precise alignment between the positioning dictionary and the 
true signal propagation model. References [15], [16] 
investigated localization scenarios where radiation sources are 
precisely located on predefined grid points, ensuring a matched 
state between the signal and the dictionary. In practical 
scenarios, it is difficult to obtain a completely accurate 
positioning dictionary, leading to a basis mismatch within the 
dictionary matrix. There are two main causes of basis mismatch 
in DPD. On one hand, the actual target often does not lie 
exactly on the predefined grid points, resulting in off-grid 
errors. On the other hand, the signal propagation model may be 
partially unknown; for example, when affected by unknown 
channel attenuation, the directly constructed sparse dictionary 
will also be partially unknown, thereby causing model 
parameter errors. In sparse reconstruction, the dictionary matrix 
should be known and accurate; otherwise, the reconstruction 
accuracy will be reduced due to basis mismatch. 
To 
address 
the 
off-grid 
problem, 
Reference 
[17] 
parameterized the steering vector dictionary using first-order 
Taylor expansion and estimate the target position via an 
alternating iteration method. Based on the concept of 
importance sampling (IS), Reference [18] introduced a novel 
non-iterative 
ML-DPD 
method 
that 
leverages 
linear 
interpolation to mitigate off-grid errors. However, the 
optimization-based approaches represented by [17] and [18] 
fundamentally fall within the deterministic processing 
paradigm. These methods treat the target position as an 
unknown, fixed parameter to be determined, seeking an optimal 
point estimation through cost function minimization while 
ignoring the inherent uncertainties in the inference process. For 
this reason, Yang et al. [19] first proposed the off-grid sparse 
Bayesian inference (OGSBI) algorithm for DOA estimation, 
which treats the target orientation to be determined as a random 
variable and assigns it a specific probability distribution. Li et 
al. [20] adapted the OGSBI framework to the localization of 
off-grid radiation sources and incorporated a grid refinement 
strategy to reduce computational load. Addressing DPD using 
DOA information in multipath environments, Hao et al. [21] 
employed an expectation-maximization (EM) approach to 
decouple the multi-dimensional optimization problem. They 
further integrated a gradient descent mechanism within the EM 
steps to iteratively update the coordinates of the grid points, 
thereby effectively eliminating off-grid errors. It is noteworthy 
that references [17-21] focus on the position offset correction of 
grid points and usually assume that the channel attenuation is a 
fixed value. However, in practical localization scenarios, 
channel attenuation is often unknown and varies with position. 
If only the position offset is corrected while ignoring the 
attenuation error, it will still lead to a mismatch between the 
dictionary and the real signal model, thereby limiting 
localization accuracy. 
To address model parameter errors, Reference [22] treated 
the propagation process at each receiving station as a dictionary, 
converted partially unknown dictionary components into part 
of the unknown signal, and leveraged the shared sparsity of 
radiation sources in different dictionaries to estimate their 
locations. However, the coupling of dictionary and signal 
uncertainties in the observed data prevents the algorithm from 
guaranteeing a unique and correct solution. In Reference [23], 
for scenarios with unknown gain and phase errors, the position 
parameters were decoupled from the gain-phase errors, 
rendering the dictionary fully known without requiring learning. 
Nevertheless, the accuracy of this method entirely depends on 
the effectiveness of the decoupling, resulting in limited 
robustness. Reference [24] introduced a learning rule for the 
channel attenuation factor (i.e., the unknown part of the 
dictionary) during the localization process, which treated the 
unknown dictionary components as parameters to be estimated 
via SBL and updated the sparse dictionary iteratively. However, 
this approach does not jointly update the attenuation factor and 
position parameters, thus constraining its learning capability. 
Methods addressing channel uncertainty in references [22-24] 
typically learn attenuation as an additional parameter 
independent of position estimation. However, they often fail to 
jointly model off-grid errors, which may cause the learning 
process to fall into local optima and result in significant 
performance degradation under high off-grid conditions. 
In summary, both off-grid errors and model parameter errors 
are critical factors affecting DPD accuracy. However, existing 
methods often optimize for only one type of error in isolation 
and fail to adequately address the basis mismatch problem 
when both errors coexist and are coupled, leading to limited 
localization accuracy. To solve the DOA estimation problem 
for hydrophone arrays with position errors, Reference [25] 
established a SBL model to formulate a joint probability 
density function for array position errors and angular grid errors 
and employed the EM algorithm to estimate the unknown 
parameters. Inspired by Reference [25], in this work we 
propose a TDOA-based DPD method with parametric 
dictionary learning (DPD-PDL) for off-grid radiation sources 
localization under unknown channel attenuation conditions.

This method unifies channel attenuation and off-grid effects as 
the deviation between the true dictionary and the constructed 
dictionary, and leverages the SBL framework to achieve joint 
optimization of parametric dictionary learning and sparse 
signal recovery. The main contributions and innovations of this 
work include: 
(1) We propose a SBL model tailored for TDOA-based 
single-antenna DPD systems and establish a joint sparse 
representation model that simultaneously characterizes off-grid 
errors and channel attenuation errors. 
(2) We formulate the DPD problem as a joint optimization of 
parametric dictionary learning and sparse signal reconstruction. 
By introducing hierarchical priors within the SBL framework, 
the 
method 
adaptively 
learns 
parametric 
dictionary 
incorporating both off-grid and model errors, thereby 
effectively improving positioning accuracy when both types of 
errors coexist. 
(3) The CRB for TDOA-based DPD are derived. Simulation 
results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves over 
20% higher positioning accuracy compared to existing methods, 
particularly under low SNR conditions.

III. TDOA-BASED DPD METHOD WITH PARAMETRIC 
DICTIONARY LEARNING 
The sparsity of targets in the spatial domain provides the 
foundation for signal reconstruction. However, the dictionary 
mismatch problem caused by off-grid effects and attenuation 
deviations poses significant challenges for conventional 
reconstruction algorithms. For the joint optimization problem 
formulated in (13), traditional 
pl -norm minimization methods 
are sensitive to noise. Therefore, this paper adopts SBL 
reconstruction algorithm, primarily due to its three advantages: 
1) Its hierarchical Bayesian model can uniformly characterize 
signal sparsity, observation noise, and dictionary parameter 
uncertainties, enabling joint inference within a unified 
framework rather than stepwise processing; 2) It automatically 
learns hyperparameters through EM, avoiding manual 
regularization tuning; 3) Compared to 
pl -norm minimization, 
SBL yields sparser and more stable solutions, demonstrating 
superior recovery performance in off-grid and perturbed 
environments. 
Compared with sparse models that only consider off-grid 
errors, we additionally incorporate a learning rule for channel 
attenuation factors. Leveraging the principles of SBL, the joint 
optimization of sparse signal reconstruction and dictionary 
parameter inference is achieved. 
The proposed method consists of two phases. In phase 1, a 
hierarchical prior model is imposed on (12), and a Laplace 
distribution containing parameter prior information is 
constructed to enhance sparsity. In phase 2, we utilize Bayesian 
inference to perform iterative updates of dictionary parameters 
given the observed values. The detailed derivation of the 
proposed method is presented below.

B. Impacts of Different SNR 
This section evaluates the localization performance of the 
algorithms under different SNR, with the SNR range set from 
-10 dB to 20 dB in steps of 5 dB. 
Fig. 7 shows the RMSE of various algorithms, with the CRB 
included for comparison. As observed, the RMSE of all 
algorithms gradually decreases with increasing SNR and 
approaches the CRB. Under low SNR conditions, two-step 
localization 
methods 
exhibit 
significantly 
degraded 
performance. The DPD-CD algorithm, which relies on a fixed 
grid with 100 m spacing, reaches a minimum localization error 
of 61.02 m at high SNR due to grid resolution limitations. In 
contrast, the proposed DPD-PDL method adaptively updates 
both the grid and channel attenuation, enabling it to better 
address energy leakage caused by off-grid sources. As a result, 
even at high SNR, DPD-PDL achieves a RMSE that surpasses 
the grid constraint, yielding lower localization errors. The 
performance improvement of DPD-PDL over DPD-OG stems 
from its consideration of attenuation effects at grid points, 
while its advantage over DPD-CD highlights the benefit of 
off-grid processing. This is particularly evident at low SNRs, 
where DPD-PDL demonstrates significantly superior accuracy. 
For instance, at SNR=-5 dB, the RMSE of DPD-PDL is 
approximately 168.32 m, compared to 211.49 m for DPD-OG 
and 248.05 m for DPD-CD—corresponding to reductions of 
about 20.4% and 32.1%, respectively. These results fully 
demonstrate the superiority and robustness of the proposed 
DPD-PDL algorithm in low SNR scenarios. 
 
Fig. 7. Localization RMSE and CRB versus SNR

comparison, Fig. 8 presents the cumulative distribution 
function (CDF) of localization errors for different algorithms at 
SNR=-5 dB. It can be seen that the proposed algorithm 
achieves a positioning error within 160 m with 80% probability, 
and within 180 m with over 95% probability. The two step 
localization algorithm performs the worst, while the proposed 
DPD-PDL method outperforms all others under low-SNR 
conditions.

C. Impacts of Different Grid Interval 
Since the grid interval is a scheduled parameter that controls 
the trade-off between complexity and accuracy in SBL 
algorithms, this section investigates its impact on localization 
performance. The SNR is fixed at 10 dB, while grid intervals of 
10 m, 25 m, 50 m, 100 m, 200 m, and 250 m are evaluated. 
Fig. 10 presents the localization RMSE of each algorithm 
under different grid intervals. As the grid interval decreases 
from 250 m to 25 m, the localization accuracy of all algorithms 
improves. This is because a smaller grid interval increases the 
probability of the algorithm capturing off-grid sources. 
Benefiting from the joint inference of attenuation coefficients 
and candidate grid points, the proposed DPD-PDL algorithm 
achieves the smallest localization error, with its RMSE 
approaching the CRB. However, when the interval is further 
reduced to 10 m, the localization error begins to increase. This 
degradation occurs because excessively small grid interval 
violates the incoherence assumption essential for CS —
specifically, the Restricted Isometry Property (RIP) condition. 
Fig. 11 illustrates the runtime of each algorithm versus grid 
interval. It is evident that smaller grid intervals lead to longer 
computational times. Due to the simultaneous learning of grid 
offsets and attenuation coefficients, DPD-PDL exhibits higher 
computational complexity compared to other methods. 
Furthermore, by comparing Fig. 10 and Fig. 11, we can 
conclude that although reducing the grid interval from 100 m to 
25 m improves localization accuracy, the resulting overly dense 
grid 
partitioning 
significantly 
increases 
computational 
complexity. Therefore, in practical applications, grid interval 
should be carefully selected to balance localization accuracy 
and computational efficiency.

VI. CONCLUSION 
Building upon the TDOA-based DPD framework, this paper 
proposes a DPD method with parametric dictionary learning for 
off-grid 
radiation 
sources. 
The 
presented 
approach 
simultaneously addresses both off-grid errors and channel 
attenuation errors by formulating the overcomplete dictionary 
matrix as a function of both grid points and attenuation 
parameters. Within the SBL framework, joint optimization of 
parametric dictionary learning and sparse signal reconstruction 
is achieved. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed 
method achieves over 20% improvement in localization 
accuracy compared to existing methods under low SNR.  
While the proposed approach shows effectiveness in 
localizing 
off-grid 
sources, 
the

focus on developing improved dictionary approximation 
strategies to accommodate more complex signal environments
