[PDF p.2] In this paper, as an alternative contribution to the task of multi-source localization under an SRP-based framework, we proposeamethodforestimatingthedirectionofarrival(DOA)of multiple broadband sound sources in far-ﬁeld scenarios based on group-sparse modeling. Group-sparse regularization, previously applied in the context of broadband source localization with co-prime arrays [45] and single directional microphones [46], [47], is here employed to model a broadband SRP map. In par- ticular, the cross-correlations used for computing the observed SRP map are modeled as a linear function of (unknown) source power spectral densities (PSDs), supported on an overcomplete search grid of candidate DOAs. Given the assumption of spatial sparsity, only a small number of candidate DOAs will have non-zero PSDs, yielding a group-sparse structure to the vector constructed by concatenating the DOA-speciﬁc PSDs from the entire search grid. We propose to estimate the full set of PSDs by solving a linear inverse problem augmented by a group-sparsity penalty. The source DOAs are then identiﬁed by the peaks of the spatial power density, i.e., the estimated direction-speciﬁc PSDs integrated over frequency. The motivation behind the proposed method lies in its potential to reveal more distinct peaks in the resulting spatial power density than those directly observed in the broadband SRP map [17], [42], [43], which can be beneﬁcial to the robustness of the DOA estimation performance when multiple sources need to be distinguished under varying acoustic conditions.

[PDF p.13] In this paper, we proposed a method for estimating the DOAs of multiple broadband sources by solving a group-sparse opti- mization problem that models a broadband SRP map as a linear function of PSDs, and where the grid resolution of the observed SRP map can be lower than the resolution of the DOA search grid used to model the candidate source PSDs. The estimation of the source DOAs is then accomplished by identifying peaks in the resulting spatial power density, i.e., the direction-speciﬁc PSDs integrated over frequency. An implementation using the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) is pre- sented, and the DOA estimation performance of the proposed method is evaluated with both simulated and experimental data. Results showed that, especially in reverberant scenarios, the pro- posed method presented an advantage in locating closely spaced sources when compared to the conventional SRP-PHAT, the group-sparse iterative covariance-based estimation (GSPICE) method, and the wideband MUSIC method with geometric averaging. Furthermore, it was observed that, for a compact microphone array, the proposed method overall maintained its performance even when using SRP maps computed with grid resolutions that are lower than the sampling requirements of the broadband SRP function. This observation, along with results obtained for limited numbers of iterations within the ADMM- scheme, indicated potential approaches to reduce the proposed method’s overall computational complexity while maintaining its DOA estimation performance. Finally, results obtained with experimental data showed the validity and applicability of the proposed method in a practical meeting room environment.
