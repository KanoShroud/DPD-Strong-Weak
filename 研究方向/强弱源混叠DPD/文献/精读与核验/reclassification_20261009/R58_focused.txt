[PDF p.2] Multi-source localization is another challenge in addition to ro- bustness and computational eﬃciency, especially in wide dynamic range scenarios, where a more powerful source can mask a weaker one. Various techniques have been recently proposed to ﬁnd mul- tiple sources [29–34] , most assuming different degrees of time- frequency sparsity on the source signals. These sparsity constraints can be applied, for example, when speech sources need to be found [35] but cannot be applied with other sources like music or noise. Furthermore, these techniques are usually based on the incoherent application of subspace DOA techniques in the time- frequency bins where just one source (or only a few sources) are present. This has two disadvantages: we lost the spatial informa- tion present in the bins where multiple sources are active and, on the other hand, subspace methods usually have high computational complexity and some of them may not work properly if correla- tion between sources exists. Finally, there is no information on the performance of these techniques when the power level difference between sources increases. Additionally, to reduce the errors in- troduced by the reverberation and by the nonstationarity of the speech signals, several tracking techniques propose to track mul- tiple sources after applying the DOA algorithm, as a subsequent processing step [36–41] .

[PDF p.11] The proposed algorithm is capable of locating multiple sources even when the second source is much weaker than the ﬁrst. The simulations made with a microphone array obtained an increase between 5 dB and 10 dB in the dynamic range in comparison with the conventional SRP-PHAT algorithm and the recordings demon- strate that the algorithm also works in real environments. This is especially important when the sources are not sparse and the weakest source will always be masked, both in time and frequency, by the strongest one. We have proof through simulations that the coarse approximation of our algorithm has a similar performance than the technique proposed in [46] but without needing to ﬁne- tune any parameter and that, working with the complete equation, we clearly outperform it. This coarse approximation, and the tech- nique proposed in [46] , has a good performance for white sources but suffers a great degradation when dealing with more complex sources like speech. It may be useful in applications where we have control over the sound sources, such as in the acoustic char- acterization of rooms, or when we want to locate noise sources that we already know are white, but for speaker localization appli- cations the complete equation should be employed.

[PDF p.11] This technique is compatible with most of the GCC-based broadband localization techniques in the state of the art; for ex- ample, when an extremely low computational cost is needed, we can just ﬁnd the maximum of each GCCs after cancelling the most powerful source and use it as second source TDOA or, if we need more robustness, we can compute the SRP with our modiﬁed GCCs. If we want to use our technique combined with the SRP algorithm, the computational cost overrun does not increase with the search space, and the different approaches that have been developed to decrease the SRP-PHAT algorithm computational cost, such as the use of GPUs or the use of different search strategies can be also employed. If a higher computational power is available, we could use the modiﬁed GCCs to obtain the PSCM and apply subspace techniques to it. After applying the DOA algorithms, most of the existing tracking algorithm can be used.
