ABSTRACT
To address the trade-off between localization accuracy and computational efficiency in direct position determination (DPD), this
paper proposes a novel hybrid optimization method that integrates correlation-domain sparsity with gradient-guided refinement.
The proposed method achieves sub-grid localization accuracy while significantly reducing computational complexity. Extensive
simulations indicate that the method maintains high precision across a wide range of signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs) and sample
sizes, achieving an order-of-magnitude reduction in runtime compared to both conventional and sparse DPD techniques.
1
Introduction
Passive localization is a cornerstone task in modern signal
processing, with critical applications spanning environmen-
tal sensing, autonomous navigation, and defence systems [1].
Conventional two-step methods follow a stepwise ‘parameter
measurement—position solution’ framework [2], but suffer from
error propagation and suboptimal performance, especially under
low SNR conditions. This has motivated the development of
DPD methods [3], which estimate emitter positions directly
from raw signals by jointly exploiting the embedded spatial
constraint, leading to significant accuracy gains with low-SNR.
In this work, we specifically target cost-sensitive, wide-area
surveillance scenarios, such as tracking UAVs or IoT emitters,
where fixed ground-based sensors and low-complexity receivers
are preferred. In such setups, time-delay-based DPD offers a
favourable trade-off between performance, scalability and system
complexity. However, existing DPD methods face two core chal-
lenges: (1) the lack of closed-form solutions necessitates expensive
grid search; and (2) the performance is constrained by grid
resolution.
2
Related Work
Existing efficient DPD methods typically trade localization
accuracy for computational efficiency. To reduce computational
complexity, methods in [4] and [5] approximate the maximum
eigenvalue of the covariance matrix using Gershgorin’s theorem
and the Frobenius norm, respectively. Notably, [5] introduces a
differentiable surrogate enabling Gauss-Newton acceleration, but
its accuracy deteriorates under low-SNR conditions.
Subsequently, sparse signal reconstruction (SSR) techniques
emerged to enhance robustness in noisy and data-limited scenar-
ios [6]. Nevertheless, grid-dependent SSR methods face inherent
resolution limitations due to quantization errors. Recent efforts
have addressed this challenge through adaptive grid refinement.
For instance, the method proposed in [7] integrates gradient
descent within an expectation-maximization (EM) framework to
dynamically adjust grid coordinates, while [8] employs sparse
Bayesian inference for off-grid correction. Although these strate-
gies effectively reduce the computational cost per iteration, they
often suffer from slow convergence rates.

Cross-domain fusion approaches represent another line of
advancement, with methods [9] and [10] extracting positional
constraints across the signal, correlation, and spatial domains.
While these approaches offer improved localization accuracy
compared to frequency-domain counterparts, their computa-
tional burden escalates rapidly with grid resolution due to the
complexity of cost functions.
Building upon these insights, this letter proposes a hybrid DPD
method designed for low-cost, wide-area surveillance systems
using fixed receivers and time delay information. The proposed
approach combines sparse correlation-domain delay mapping
with a continuous-domain refinement mechanism. Specifically,
it constructs high-resolution delay spectra via sparse recon-
struction, interpolates them into a differentiable representation,
and applies gradient-guided updates to iteratively refine emit-
ter positions. This fusion of discrete and continuous inference
obtains sub-grid localization accuracy, bypassing grid limita-
tions and achieving both robustness and efficiency in low SNR
environments where conventional methods fail.

Complexity Analysis
The overall complexity of the proposed algorithm consists of
the time complexity T and the space complexity M. The time
complexity involves three stages: (1) signal pre-processing: Per-
forming N-point FFT for L sensors and computing cross-spectra
for P station pairs results in O(LNlog N), where theO(PN)term is
negligible underN ≫P; (2) sparse spectral reconstruction: Each
pair of stations per iteration requires a matrix multiplication of
O(G2
𝜏𝑁) and the inversion cost O(G3
𝜏) which is ignored for N
≫Gτ. The total cost across P pairs of stations and I1 iterations
is O(𝑃𝐼1G2
𝜏𝑁); and (3) delay-to-space mapping and refinement:
hybrid-guided optimization over I2 iterations adds O(I2P), which
is minor. In typical scenarios where N ≫Gτ ≫P > L, the
dominant term leads to 𝑇≈O(𝑃𝐼1G2
𝜏𝑁). The spline interpolation
in Equation (10) significantly reduces Gτ, and the proposed
fast-converging optimization algorithm keeps the number of
iterations very small. Regarding space complexity, memory usage
is mainly due to the storage of frequency-domain signals and
over-complete dictionary, giving M = O(LN + GτN).

Numerical Simulations
The simulation setup consists of four stationary sensors arranged
in a 1 km×1 km square, monitoring an emitter randomly located
within the surveillance area, such as 𝐮= [53.5, −86.5]T. The
emitter’s parameters are set as fs = 100MHz and fc = 1GHz. Three
benchmark methods are considered: TDOA-WLS in [2], DPD-
SML in [3], and DPD-Sparse in [9]. All compared algorithms
utilize a multiresolution grid search over a 200m × 200m area
with an initial grid spacing of 5 m. The proposed method
integrates sparsity priors and gradient-driven iteration, starting
from a coarser initial resolution of 10 m.
Monte Carlo simulations are conducted over 200 trials. Figures 3
and 4 depict the localization RMSE and run time versus SNR
with the sample size fixed at N = 1000. As shown in Figure 3,
the proposed method obtains significant performance gains in
low-SNR regimes. Specifically, it maintains an RMSE below 0.1
m at –5 dB, outperforming DPD-SML and DPD-Sparse by 81%
and 79%, respectively. Its performance degradation threshold lies
between –10 dB and –5 dB, while DPD-SML and DPD-Sparse

7
Conclusion
An efficient DPD method is proposed, leveraging sparse struc-
tures in the cross-correlation domain and gradient-driven refine-
ment to achieve accurate and efficient emitter localization. By
avoiding grid resolution limitations and accelerating conver-
gence, the proposed method enhances both localization accuracy
and efficiency. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed
approach significantly outperforms conventional and sparse DPD
methods under low-SNR conditions. And the proposed method
maintains superior localization performance while reducing
runtime across varied sample sizes. Overall, this letter offers
a competitive solution for real-time passive localization in
challenging environments.
