[PDF p.1] ABSTRACT To address the trade-off between localization accuracy and computational efficiency in direct position determination (DPD), this paper proposes a novel hybrid optimization method that integrates correlation-domain sparsity with gradient-guided refinement. The proposed method achieves sub-grid localization accuracy while significantly reducing computational complexity. Extensive simulations indicate that the method maintains high precision across a wide range of signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs) and sample sizes, achieving an order-of-magnitude reduction in runtime compared to both conventional and sparse DPD techniques.

[PDF p.1] Passive localization is a cornerstone task in modern signal processing, with critical applications spanning environmen- tal sensing, autonomous navigation, and defence systems [1]. Conventional two-step methods follow a stepwise ‘parameter measurement—position solution’ framework [2], but suffer from error propagation and suboptimal performance, especially under low SNR conditions. This has motivated the development of DPD methods [3], which estimate emitter positions directly from raw signals by jointly exploiting the embedded spatial constraint, leading to significant accuracy gains with low-SNR. In this work, we specifically target cost-sensitive, wide-area surveillance scenarios, such as tracking UAVs or IoT emitters, where fixed ground-based sensors and low-complexity receivers are preferred. In such setups, time-delay-based DPD offers a favourable trade-off between performance, scalability and system complexity. However, existing DPD methods face two core chal- lenges: (1) the lack of closed-form solutions necessitates expensive grid search; and (2) the performance is constrained by grid resolution.

[PDF p.1] Subsequently, sparse signal reconstruction (SSR) techniques emerged to enhance robustness in noisy and data-limited scenar- ios [6]. Nevertheless, grid-dependent SSR methods face inherent resolution limitations due to quantization errors. Recent efforts have addressed this challenge through adaptive grid refinement. For instance, the method proposed in [7] integrates gradient descent within an expectation-maximization (EM) framework to dynamically adjust grid coordinates, while [8] employs sparse Bayesian inference for off-grid correction. Although these strate- gies effectively reduce the computational cost per iteration, they often suffer from slow convergence rates.

[PDF p.1] Existing efficient DPD methods typically trade localization accuracy for computational efficiency. To reduce computational complexity, methods in [4] and [5] approximate the maximum eigenvalue of the covariance matrix using Gershgorin’s theorem and the Frobenius norm, respectively. Notably, [5] introduces a differentiable surrogate enabling Gauss-Newton acceleration, but its accuracy deteriorates under low-SNR conditions.

[PDF p.2] Building upon these insights, this letter proposes a hybrid DPD method designed for low-cost, wide-area surveillance systems using fixed receivers and time delay information. The proposed approach combines sparse correlation-domain delay mapping with a continuous-domain refinement mechanism. Specifically, it constructs high-resolution delay spectra via sparse recon- struction, interpolates them into a differentiable representation, and applies gradient-guided updates to iteratively refine emit- ter positions. This fusion of discrete and continuous inference obtains sub-grid localization accuracy, bypassing grid limita- tions and achieving both robustness and efficiency in low SNR environments where conventional methods fail.

[PDF p.5] An efficient DPD method is proposed, leveraging sparse struc- tures in the cross-correlation domain and gradient-driven refine- ment to achieve accurate and efficient emitter localization. By avoiding grid resolution limitations and accelerating conver- gence, the proposed method enhances both localization accuracy and efficiency. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed approach significantly outperforms conventional and sparse DPD methods under low-SNR conditions. And the proposed method maintains superior localization performance while reducing runtime across varied sample sizes. Overall, this letter offers a competitive solution for real-time passive localization in challenging environments.
