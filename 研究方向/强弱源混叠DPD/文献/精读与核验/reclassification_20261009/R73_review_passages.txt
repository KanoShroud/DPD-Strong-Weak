[PDF p.1] Abstract Mutual coupling (MC) between array elements can lead to severe accuracy degradation in direction‐of‐arrival (DOA) estimation. In this study, an off‐grid compressed sensing (CS) approach for DOA estimation in the presence of unknown MC is proposed using block Newtonised orthogonal matching pursuit (NOMP). First, the model of DOA estimation with MC is block sparsely represented to fully utilise the whole array aperture. Then, a block NOMP algorithm with multiple measurement vectors (MMV) is developed to achieve the estimates of DOA, in which the Newton refinement procedure is presented by deriving the first‐ and second‐order derivatives of the block steering matrix with respect to the angle grid. Compared with the subspace‐based and sparse recovery‐based methods with MC, the proposed block NOMP approach has robustness in a small number of snapshots and owns no grid‐mismatch effect. Meanwhile, in comparison to several existing off‐grid DOA estimation methods with MC, it can achieve higher esti- mation accuracy and similarly low computational complexity. The superior performance of the proposed approach is shown via numerical simulations.

[PDF p.1] As a key technology in array signal processing, direction‐of‐ arrival (DOA) estimation has received extensive attention in ra- dar, sonar, communication, and radio astronomy etc [1]. Many classical DOA estimation methods have been put forward over the past few decades such as Capon, MUSIC and ESPRIT etc [2]. In DOA estimation, we usually assume the ideal situation that the array manifold is exactly known and has a determined expression. However, such assumption is invalid in practice due to array mutual coupling (MC), which is usually caused by in- teractions among sensor array elements. Unknown MC may cause severe distortion and degradation in estimation accuracy [3]. To eliminate the MC effect, many array calibration tech- niques have been proposed. Most of them [4–15] are based on subspace techniques (e.g., MUSIC, ESPRIT). [PDF p.2] Orthogonal matching pursuit (OMP) [24] is an efficiently iterative greedy algorithm, which chooses the column of observation matrix that is most correlated to the current re- sidual at each step. Unfortunately, as an on‐grid compressed sensing (CS) method, the OMP suffers from the grid‐mismatch effect. Newtonised orthogonal matching pursuit (NOMP), proposed in [25] for frequency estimation, can overcome the disadvantage of the OMP method by the Newton refinement procedure and enjoys low computational complexity. Most importantly, NOMP does not need the adjustment of regular- isation parameters.

[PDF p.2] Motivated by [25], in this study, we address the off‐grid CS problem for DOA estimation in the presence of un- known MC, and propose a block NOMP algorithm. First, we represent the signal model of DOA estimation as a block sparse form in order to fully utilise the whole array aperture in the presence of MC, where each potential DOA corre- sponds to a block matrix. Then, we extend the original NOMP to its block sparse version with multiple measurement vectors (MMV) for DOA estimation. In the proposed block NOMP algorithm, we estimate the initial DOA by finding the most similar DOA value in the predefined block grid of DOA based on the maximum likelihood algorithm. Then, we gradually refine the estimates of DOAs by a block Newton method.

[PDF p.2] The main contribution of our study is as follows: (a) we propose a block NOMP approach for off‐grid DOA estimation in the presence of unknown mutual coupling. The proposed approach can overcome the grid‐mismatch effect of the exist- ing sparse recovery methods and has relatively low computa- tional complexity; (b) unlike the original NOMP algorithm in [25], the proposed block NOMP algorithm is applicable to the MMV case, whose single‐source observation model contains a block steering matrix rather than a steering vector, thus the matrix product rather than vector product is utilised. In addi- tion, the first‐ and second‐order derivatives of the block steering matrix with respect to angle grid are derived in the block Newton refinement procedure.

As shown in Figure 2, the pro- posed approach has the lowest RMSE among the six methods, indicating that it owns the highest estimation accuracy via the block NOMP algorithm. The subspace‐based MC‐ESPRIT and MC‐MUSIC‐like methods have moderate values of RMSE. The two sparse recovery methods, MC‐l1‐Norm and MC‐Block‐l1‐Norm, have the higher RMSEs and worse per- formance in accuracy. Their RMSEs are about 0:4° at SNR = 16dB, which is equal to the ideal RMSE via direct grid‐ based sparse recovery methods, showing that they suffer from the existence of grid‐mismatch effect. MC‐l1‐Norm has lower accuracy than MC‐Block‐l1‐Norm due to the partial utilisation of the whole array information. The MC‐Off‐Grid‐BCS method, which uses Bayesian compressed sensing (BCS) and first‐order Taylor expansion, has lower RMSE than MC‐ ESPRIT and MC‐MUSIC‐like methods, and has no grid‐ mismatch effect. However, it still has lower accuracy than the proposed approach.
