[PDF p.1] Abstract — Single-snapshot direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation is fundamental in automotive MIMO radar, where fast-chirp waveforms typically provide only one measurement per range–Doppler cell. In this regime, classical subspace techniques such as MUSIC and ESPRIT suffer from rank deficiency, while grid-based sparse recovery methods are affected by basis mismatch and increased complexity for large, irregular sparse planar arrays. This paper proposes a gridless single-snapshot DOA estimator operating on the continuous steering manifold of general sparse planar virtual arrays. A coarse-to-fine matched-filter search on the physically consistent array manifold provides geometry-aware initialization. The detected components are subsequently refined via a Gauss–Newton scheme with closed-form Jacobians and embedded in a lightweight RELAX-style cyclic update to mitigate multi-target coupling. To resolve merged components in high dynamic-range scenarios, a local one-versus-two model comparison based on the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) is employed. Model order is determined through a statistically grounded generalized likelihood ratio test (GLRT)–based stopping rule that controls the target overestimation probability.

A computationally lighter alternative is local continuous-domain refinement: a coarse initialization followed by Newton or Gauss–Newton updates to achieve off-grid accuracy. Such refinement is well established in 1-D line-spectrum estimation (e.g., Newtonized OMP or NOMP) with near Cramér–Rao Bound (CRB) behavior in suitable regimes [6]. [PDF p.1] This paper develops a fast gridless single-snapshot estimator that refines DOA parameters directly on the continuous steering manifold of general sparse planar virtual arrays. Initialization is performed via a coarse-to-fine matched-filter search on the physically consistent manifold, followed by Gauss–Newton refinement with closed-form Jacobians and a lightweight RELAX-style cyclic update to mitigate multi-target coupling. Merged components are resolved through a local one-versus-two split test based on the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) [11], and the number of detected components is controlled using a generalized likelihood ratio test (GLRT)–based stopping rule consistent with Newtonized pursuit methods [7]. Simulations on automotive-scale MIMO sparse arrays demonstrate reliable performance in high dynamic-range scenarios and sub-beamwidth separability. Theoretical analysis further confirms that the computational complexity scales linearly with the number of virtual elements and coarse grid evaluations, followed by low-dimensional refinement overhead.
