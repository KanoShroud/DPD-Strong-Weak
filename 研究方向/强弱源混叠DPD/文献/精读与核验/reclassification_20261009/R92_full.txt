Theory and Practice of Light-Weight Sequential
SBL Algorithm: An Alternative to OMP
Rohan R. Pote
, Member, IEEE, and Bhaskar D. Rao
, Life Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—We propose a low complexity forward selection
algorithm for the sparse signal recovery (SSR) problem based on
the sparse Bayesian learning (SBL) formulation. The proposed
algorithm, called as light-weight sequential SBL (LWS-SBL),
offers an alternative to the widely used iterative and greedy
algorithm known as orthogonal matching pursuit (OMP). In
contrast to OMP, which models the unknown sparse vector as
a deterministic variable, the same is modeled as a stochastic
variable within LWS-SBL. Speciﬁcally, the proposed algorithm
is derived from the stochastic maximum likelihood estimation
framework, and it iteratively selects columns that maximally
increase the likelihood. We derive efﬁcient recursive procedure to
update the internal parameters of the algorithm, and maintain a
similar asymptotic computational complexity as OMP. Additional
two perspectives, one based on array processing beamforming
interpretations and the other based on a local high-resolution
analysis, are provided to understand the underlying differences
in the mechanisms of the two algorithms. They reveal avenues
where LWS-SBL improves over OMP. These are veriﬁed in
the numerical section in terms of improved support recovery
performance. Similar to the counterparts in OMP, for SSR
problems involving parametric dictionaries, the ﬂexibility of the
proposed approach is demonstrated by extending LWS-SBL to
recover multi-dimensional parameters, and in a gridless manner.
Index Terms—Sparse Bayesian learning, sparse signal re-
covery, fast algorithm, beamforming, gridless, multidimensional
parameter estimation.

Bayes framework will be discussed more in the next section.

A. Relevant Prior Work
Sparse Bayesian learning was proposed as a Bayesian tech-
nique for obtaining sparse solutions to regression and classiﬁ-
cation tasks in [18]. In [15], [27], a sequential and fast mech-
anism to maximize the marginalized likelihood was proposed.
Expectation maximization SBL (EM-SBL) was proposed for
the SSR problem in [14]. A comparative study about the effect
of different weight distributions on ﬁnding sparse solutions, in
EM-SBL, OMP and Basis Pursuit algorithms was presented in
[30]. A detailed comparison of the Type-II objective function
resulting from the SBL framework and the Type-I framework
resulting from the MAP formulation was provided in [19] along
with the advantages of the Type-II objective function. A com-
pressive sensing algorithm was proposed in [31] and a multitask
compressive sensing algorithm was proposed in [32], based on
the fast approach in [15]. The model considered in [32] is akin to
the multiple measurement vector (MMV) model considered in
[33], [34]. In [35], a fast variational SBL approach is proposed
by sequentially maximizing a variational lower bound, similar
to the approach in [15]. The fast variational SBL approach was
extended to the block sparse model in [36]. A greedy Bayesian
inference algorithm that captures the correlation among latent
signal vectors was proposed in [37] based on [15]. An EM
based non-greedy approach for the temporally correlated source
vectors problem was proposed in [38]. The approach in this
paper relates to the sequential marginal likelihood maximiza-
tion approach of [15]. In contrast to [15], where the authors
describe a general framework for sequential maximization al-
lowing for deletion and re-estimation, in this paper a speciﬁc
forward selection algorithm is derived. The proposed algorithm
is comparable with the widely used greedy algorithm, OMP, in
terms of computational complexity. Furthermore, novel insights
are drawn based on this benchmark which highlight critical
scenarios where the performances of the two algorithms show
signiﬁcant differences.
A forward-backward algorithm based on the SBL objective is
presented in [39] along with interesting connections to stepwise
regression and the matching pursuit class of algorithms. An efﬁ-
cient SBL implementation using Gibbs sampling was proposed
in [40], and using belief propagation was proposed in [41]. A
fast algorithm based on space alternating variational estimations
[42] and inverse-free SBL using relaxed evidence lower bound
[43] was proposed in [44]. A generalized approximate message
passing-based low complexity algorithm for SBL was proposed
in [45]. The work prevents the need for matrix inversion in the
expectation step in EM-SBL. The sequential SBL framework
has been exploited in the works of [46], [47] for frequency
estimation and in [48] for channel estimation with considerable
gains. Our goal in contrast to these works is to provide a general
framework making it easy to adopt to different applications.
Specializing to applications and exploiting application-speciﬁc
structure does provide enhancements and is certainly desirable.
This work builds upon the initial conference version [28].

II. LIGHT-WEIGHT SEQUENTIAL SBL ALGORITHM
In this work, we focus on an iterative approach to ﬁnd
sparse solutions to (1) while maintaining a low computational
complexity. Within an iterative scheme the goal is to identify
the support over iterations. Greed-based iterative approaches
optimize a certain objective function, L(.), at every iteration
with respect to candidate support elements. We focus on the
greedy-iterative paradigm and develop an algorithm that adds
one column of Φ at every iteration. As a benchmark we consider
the widely used OMP algorithm which sequentially solves a
least squares objective. Next, we discuss the SBL formulation
which motivates the objective function adopted in the pro-
posed algorithm. Without loss of generality, we assume that
the columns of Φ are normalized throughout in this paper i.e.,
||φj||2 = 1, where φj denotes the j-th column of Φ.
A. Sparse Bayesian Learning
SBL is a Bayesian technique to compute a sparse decomposi-
tion of measurements y. SBL models x as a random variable, in
contrast to the OMP algorithm [16], [20] where x is modeled as
a deterministic unknown. More speciﬁcally, in the basic variant,
a parameterized Gaussian prior is imposed on x with mean zero
and uncorrelated components. In other words, x ∼CN(0, Γ)
where Γ is a diagonal matrix; let diag(Γ) = γ. Under the zero
mean Gaussian noise assumption, y ∼CN(0, ΦΓΦH + λI),
where λ denotes the noise variance. The SBL approach is to
solve (1) in the hyperparameter γ-space, post marginalization
with respect to x. This approach is known as Type-II estimation
framework, compared to Type-I where the problem is solved in
x space after marginalization with respect to γ [49] (e.g., Lasso
algorithm [17]). The beneﬁts of using the Type-II objective
function over a Type-I objective function were discussed in
[19]. The emphasis, in the optimization of the Type-II objective,
was on SBL algorithms that jointly optimize the hyperparame-
ters as in EM-SBL. Note that the Gaussian density imposition
on x is not a limitation, and the method generalizes well to the
case when x was in fact drawn from a non-Gaussian density
(e.g., see Section VI-A. in [14]). The hyperparameter γ is
estimated by minimizing the negative marginal log-likelihood
function [14], [18]
min
γ≥0,λ≥0L(γ, λ) := log det Σy + yHΣ−1
y y.
(3)
where, Σy = ΦΓΦH + λI. (3) is a non-convex problem in
(γ, λ). In this work, we initialize with γj = 0, ∀j ∈{1, . . . , n},
and add a column with index l that minimizes the negative
log-likelihood, the most. Thus, similar to OMP, the proposed
algorithm selects one column per iteration. However, in contrast
to OMP, each iteration in the proposed scheme optimizes the
maximum likelihood based cost function in (3). The following
remark is in order.
Remark 1: The sequential SBL approach in [15] is a frame-
work and does not provide a speciﬁc algorithm, but rather
suggestions (see Section 4 in [15]) for developing variants with
different options, e.g. adding or deleting selected columns, or
modifying the variances of the columns already selected. To
ensure a computational complexity comparable to OMP, we
develop a speciﬁc algorithm, which like OMP, runs the sequen-
tial steps for K iterations only; K denotes the desired support
size. An interesting paper utilizing the framework in [15] is
[39] where the Relevance Matching Pursuit algorithm is derived
which includes both addition and deletion of columns. The
limiting variant, as noise variance goes to zero, is shown to be
connected to the criteria used in stepwise regression. Based on
the framework in [15], it is clear that other interesting variants
are possible and likely to generate some interesting new greedy-
search algorithms. Our emphasis is low complexity comparable
to OMP.
Next we derive steps to iteratively optimize (3).

V. GRIDLESS LWS-SBL ALGORITHM
We now discuss the case when Φ has a parametric repre-
sentation, and extend LWS-SBL to perform gridless parameter
estimation. Consider the following parametric data model

tinuous domain. K denotes the number of active sources. Model
assumptions made in (1) are also applicable in (33). The above
problem is ubiquitous, with applications such as line spectral
estimation [54] and direction-of-arrival (DoA) estimation [55]
for narrowband signals; we emphasize the latter as means for
exposition.
The proposed Algorithm 1 in its present form is not amenable
to the parametric problem in (33). Mathematically, the approach
can be readily developed by modifying equation (7) to reﬂect
optimization over the continuous parameter space rather than
the index set. However, this step when implemented will re-
quire a grid search though the grid is not predetermined. As
a practical implementation of this concept, we adopt a two-
step approach where in step-1 we recover an on-grid support
set using Algorithm 1. This is followed by step-2 where we
locally optimize the recovered grid points over a smaller pa-
rameter space. This step is inspired by a previous work by the
authors [56] on batch EM-SBL where all components of γ are
simultaneously updated. In contrast to [56], this paper proposes
a ﬁxed iterations strategy to identify a sparse solution, with
an emphasis on computational complexity reduction. Such a
two step approach offers ﬂexibility, as the second step can be
specialized to any parametric measurement model, while the
ﬁrst step remains generally applicable. This effort also offers
a direct counterpart to the existing work that extends OMP to
support gridless parameter estimation [57].
An approach that takes advantage of the parametric model
from the start is another suitable alternative. Algorithmically,
one of the key differences will be in how the parameter space
is explored for optimization. For an approach that exploits the
parametric model right away at the ﬁrst step itself for frequency
estimation, we refer interested readers to [46], [47]. Similarly,
an approach has been recently developed for channel estimation
in ultra-wide-band (UWB) environments and the result is a state
of the art high resolution channel estimator [48]. Embedding the
approach in an application has the beneﬁt of being able to take

In this section we study the empirical performance of the
algorithms presented in this work, and compare them with

VII. CONCLUSION
A novel low complexity algorithm for the sparse signal recov-
ery problem is proposed based on the Sparse Bayesian Learning
(SBL) formulation, named as Light-Weight Sequential SBL
algorithm. The proposed algorithm recovers support over it-
erations, similar to OMP, but unlike OMP it is based on the
stochastic maximum likelihood estimation framework. Support
vectors are added to the recovery set that gradually increases
the likelihood over iterations. Efﬁcient recursive iterations are
developed with the computational complexity similar to OMP.
A beamformer and metric-based perspective is developed that
can provide a fundamental framework to classify and under-
stand different sparse signal recovery algorithms. Under this
framework, LWS-SBL and OMP are analyzed to understand
their subtle differences. Numerical results are provided that both
verify the theory and demonstrate the improvement achieved
using LWS-SBL. Gridless extensions of the proposed algorithm
are provided with applications to both 1D parametric model and
multi-dimensional harmonic retrieval problem.

Bhaskar D. Rao (Life Fellow, IEEE) received the
B.Tech. degree in electronics and electrical com-
munication engineering from the Indian Institute of
Technology, Kharagpur, India, in 1979, and the M.S.
and Ph.D. degrees from the University of South-
ern California, Los Angeles, in 1981 and 1983,
respectively. He has been teaching and conducting
research with the University of California in San
Diego, La Jolla. Since 1983, where he is currently
a Professor Emeritus and a Distinguished Professor
of the Graduate Division, Electrical and Computer
Engineering Department. He has also been the Holder of the Ericsson
Endowed Chair in wireless access networks and a Distinguished Professor
and the Director of the Center for Wireless Communications (2008–2011).
His research interests include areas of statistical signal processing, estimation
theory, optimization theory and machine learning, with applications to digital
communications, speech signal processing, and biomedical signal processing.
He is a pioneer in the theory and use of sparsity in signal processing
applications. Since co-authoring the ﬁrst paper on the seminal FOCUSS
algorithm, in 1992, he has been driving the ﬁeld of sparsity forward, including
co-organizing the ﬁrst special session on sparsity with ICASSP 1998 entitled
“SPEC-DSP: Signal Processing with Sparseness Constraint.” His work has
received several paper awards, including the 2012 Signal Processing Society
(SPS) best paper award for the paper “An Empirical Bayesian Strategy for
Solving the Simultaneous Sparse Approximation Problem,” with David P.
Wipf and the Stephen O. Rice Prize Paper Award in the ﬁeld of communication
systems for the paper “Network Duality for Multiuser MIMO Beamforming
Networks and Applications,” with B. Song and R. L. Cruz. He was elected
fellow of IEEE in 2000 for his contributions to the statistical analysis of
subspace algorithms for harmonic retrieval. He received the IEEE Signal
Processing Society Technical Achievement Award in 2016 and was the
recipient of the 2023 IEEE SPS Norbert Wiener Society Award. He has
been a member of the Statistical Signal and Array Processing Technical
Committee, the Signal Processing Theory and Methods Technical Committee,
the Communications Technical Committee of the IEEE Signal Processing
Society, SPS Fellow Evaluation Committee (2023–2024) and was the Chair
of the Machine Learning for Signal Processing Technical Committee (2019–
2020).
