[PDF p.1] Abstract—We propose a low complexity forward selection algorithm for the sparse signal recovery (SSR) problem based on the sparse Bayesian learning (SBL) formulation. The proposed algorithm, called as light-weight sequential SBL (LWS-SBL), offers an alternative to the widely used iterative and greedy algorithm known as orthogonal matching pursuit (OMP). In contrast to OMP, which models the unknown sparse vector as a deterministic variable, the same is modeled as a stochastic variable within LWS-SBL. Speciﬁcally, the proposed algorithm is derived from the stochastic maximum likelihood estimation framework, and it iteratively selects columns that maximally increase the likelihood. We derive efﬁcient recursive procedure to update the internal parameters of the algorithm, and maintain a similar asymptotic computational complexity as OMP. Additional two perspectives, one based on array processing beamforming interpretations and the other based on a local high-resolution analysis, are provided to understand the underlying differences in the mechanisms of the two algorithms. They reveal avenues where LWS-SBL improves over OMP. These are veriﬁed in the numerical section in terms of improved support recovery performance. Similar to the counterparts in OMP, for SSR problems involving parametric dictionaries, the ﬂexibility of the proposed approach is demonstrated by extending LWS-SBL to recover multi-dimensional parameters, and in a gridless manner.

OMP is a widely employed [25], [26] iterative technique in this category that recovers x deterministically. Each iteration freezes the past recovered support and optimizes (2) with respect to one additional column. The non-zero entries in x of the past recovered support is also ﬁxed during this step. The contributions made in this work are summarized below:

[PDF p.2] 1) Utilizing the framework developed in [15], [27], a light weight sequential sparse Bayesian learning (LWS-SBL) algorithm is proposed as an alternative to OMP. In con- trast to OMP, it is derived from the Type-II estima- tion framework and the stochastic maximum likelihood objective. 2) Computationally efﬁcient steps based on recursive im- plementations are developed to maintain a complexity similar to that of OMP. 3) Two perspectives, one based on a beamforming interpre- tation and another based on a high-resolution analysis, are provided to study LWS-SBL in comparison to OMP. These perspectives highlight key differences between the two algorithms, and augments the prior work by the au- thors in [28]. The analysis also highlights avenues where LWS-SBL improves over the OMP algorithm; more the- oretical justiﬁcations in this regard are provided. 4) For parametric dictionaries, the underlying goal of esti- mating parameter in a gridless manner is identiﬁed and a gridless extension of proposed LWS-SBL algorithm is provided as an alternative to the counterparts for the extensions of OMP. In comparison to our work in [28], the computational step uses Newton updates and thus is more economical. 5) Flexibility and generality of the proposed approach to the multi-dimensional harmonic retrieval problem is demon- strated by describing the corresponding LWS-SBL exten- sion and its gridless counterpart. The ﬂexibility and sim- plicity of the extension to parametric dictionaries should enable extension to other application scenarios with ease and enable easy adoption. The ideas presented in this work are empirically analyzed in the Section VI. The overarching goal of this work is to develop a general algorithm that practitioners can use with less effort. This is an important consideration for adoption of algorithms, as evident from the success of OMP. Thus, adopting a general low complexity SBL algorithm for sparse signal recovery, and further tuning it for parameterized dictionaries serves this pur- pose. [PDF p.3] SBL is a Bayesian technique to compute a sparse decomposi- tion of measurements y. SBL models x as a random variable, in contrast to the OMP algorithm [16], [20] where x is modeled as a deterministic unknown. More speciﬁcally, in the basic variant, a parameterized Gaussian prior is imposed on x with mean zero and uncorrelated components. In other words, x ∼CN(0, Γ) where Γ is a diagonal matrix; let diag(Γ) = γ. Under the zero mean Gaussian noise assumption, y ∼CN(0, ΦΓΦH + λI), where λ denotes the noise variance. The SBL approach is to solve (1) in the hyperparameter γ-space, post marginalization with respect to x. This approach is known as Type-II estimation framework, compared to Type-I where the problem is solved in x space after marginalization with respect to γ [49] (e.g., Lasso algorithm [17]). The beneﬁts of using the Type-II objective function over a Type-I objective function were discussed in [19]. The emphasis, in the optimization of the Type-II objective, was on SBL algorithms that jointly optimize the hyperparame- ters as in EM-SBL. Note that the Gaussian density imposition on x is not a limitation, and the method generalizes well to the case when x was in fact drawn from a non-Gaussian density (e.g., see Section VI-A. in [14]).
