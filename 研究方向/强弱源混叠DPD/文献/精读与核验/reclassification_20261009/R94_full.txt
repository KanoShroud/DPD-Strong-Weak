Efficient 2-D Direction of Arrival Estimation
Using Adaptive Grid Integrated Dictionaries
Yanan Wu , Member, IEEE, Xiaotong Tu , Member, IEEE, and Bing Zheng, Member, IEEE
Abstract—This article proposes a novel 2-D direction-of-
arrival (DOA) estimation method based on sparse constraints
and adaptive optimization, aiming to address issues such
as grid mismatch and high computational complexity in tra-
ditional DOA estimation techniques. In contrast to using a
traditional grid over distinct angles, combined angle informa-
tion atoms are adopted to construct the dictionary, using a
power-based covariance matrix fitting method to estimate the
noise power and then to select the active regions. To com-
pensate for off-grid bias effects, a 2-D dictionary learning
procedure is proposed, which is able to yield accurate esti-
mates even in single snapshot scenarios, without assuming prior information about the number of sources. Numerical
simulations illustrate the preferable performance of the proposed method as compared to recent 2-D DOA estimation
algorithms.
Index Terms— 2-D dictionary learning, 2-D direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation, adaptive optimization, integrated
dictionary.
I. INTRODUCTION
D
ETERMINING the azimuth and elevation angles of
various forms of reflectors is a commonly occurring
problem in a wide range of applications, enabling precise
spatial location information [2]. Various classical uniform
linear array (ULA) methods have been extended to allow
for 2-D direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation, including the
classical MUSIC [3] and ESPRIT [4] algorithms. As is well
known, the estimation performance of these methods degrades
significantly as the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) decreases,
when limited data samples are available, when the number of
sources is unknown, or when the background noise not suffi-
ciently white. These shortcoming has led to the development
of several alternative approaches.

Recently, sparsity-driven approaches have attracted notable
attention as an alternative to enforce sparse solutions. It has
been shown that convex relaxation algorithms, such as
the LASSO-based DOA estimation algorithms [5], [6],
demonstrate
excellent
performance
for
grid-based
DOA
estimation. However, these methods strongly rely on that the
source is on the assumed grid, rapidly losing performance as
the source moves off the grid. Several other approaches have
been proposed. A cross-covariance matrix of an L-shaped
sparse array method was proposed in [7] to increase the
degrees of freedom (DOF), though it requires a strictly
defined array structure. The eigenvalue decomposition-based
estimation methods in [8] and [9] and the 2-D grids searching
methods in [10] and [11] typically require fine grid resolutions
to maintain accuracy [12], [13], as does the Newtonized
orthogonal matching pursuit (NOMP) algorithm introduced
in [14]. Extending these approaches further, the the CFAR
based Newtonized OMP (NOMP-CFAR) algorithm [15]
further enhances robustness in line spectral estimation for
radar and sonar applications. By incorporating CFAR detection
into the iterative Newton refinement process, NOMP-CFAR
not only mitigates off-grid bias but also maintains a constant
false alarm rate under unknown and varying noise conditions,
making it well-suited for practical signal processing tasks.
As an alternative, grid-free techniques, such as atomic
norm minimization (ANM)-based algorithms [16], [17], [18],
eliminate the dependency on a fixed grid. Initially devel-
oped for single-snapshot problems, these algorithms offer

cases and further improve with the availability of multiple
snapshots. For example, the gridless DOA estimation method
introduced in [19] primarily considers static targets, and addi-
tional consideration are required to allow for tracking dynamic
targets. In [20], two gridless methods were proposed that
provides a new perspective in the field of DOA trajectory
localization using polynomial and harmonic trajectory models,
but the probability of detection varies significantly with the
used threshold adjustments, particularly at low SNRs. Overall,
in cases when the sample support is low, and/or for cases with
low SNR, most available methods struggle to yield reliable
estimates. In this article, striving to reduce the likelihood
of missing off-grid components and to reduce the required
computational complexity, we propose a 2-D DOA estimation
method that allows for single snapshot data without requiring
prior knowledge of the number of sources. Instead of using a
conventional dictionary, treating the azimuth, θ, and elevation,
φ, angles as separated atoms, we here use a dictionary over
the combined angles sin θ sin φ and sin θ cos φ to lower the
dimension of the dictionary, effectively reducing the dictionary
size. Inspired by the 1-D-integrated dictionary framework [21],
we introduced an integrated 2-D dictionary employing a
power-based covariance fitting formulation [22], [23], such
that the dictionary atoms are formed over a continuous range,
thereby reducing grid mismatch effects and enhancing robust-
ness in dynamic noisy environments. To further improve the
computational efficiency, the estimated noise powers obtained
using the integrated dictionary are then employed to form an
adaptive threshold allowing only active atoms to be retained
in each iteration. This efficiently shrinks the active regions in
each iteration, substantially lowering the overall complexity.
Additionally, an adaptive grid dictionary learning step is
proposed to compensate for the off-grid bias, ensuring accurate
DOA estimates across varying signal conditions. We show
by numerical simulations that the proposed algorithm can
achieve preferable performance as compared to the state-of-
the-art, at a low computational cost, for off-grid sources, while
being robust to the number of used bands and to the choice
of the used regularization parameter, without requiring prior
knowledge of the number of signals impinging on the array.
As shown, the proposed method allows for reliable DOA
estimates from a single snapshot, enabling an efficient tracking
of moving targets.
The rest of this article is organized as follows. Section II
introduces the signal model for the rectangular array 2-D
DOA estimation problem. In Section III, the novel angle infor-
mation integrated dictionary is introduced and the efficiency
of using the estimated power as a threshold retaining the
regions of interest is shown. In Section IV, a computation-
ally efficient solution to solve the proposed problem using
the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) is
derived and analyzed for both the single and multiple snapshot
cases. In Section V, we developed an adaptive grid dictionary
learning step to compensate further for the off-grid bias,
whereas Section VI summarizes the computational complexity
of the proposed method. We evaluate the performance of the

VIII. CONCLUSION
In this article, a novel 2-D DOA estimation method is
proposed, wherein a novel 2-D-integrated dictionary is con-
structed to reduce the computational cost. The dictionary’s
active regions are selected using an initial coarse estimate
of the noise power, allowing for a refined high-resolution
estimate without a substantial increase in computational com-
plexity. Additionally, an efficient dictionary learning strategy
is introduced to compensate for the inevitable off-grid bias.
Numerical results illustrate the proposed method’s preferable
performance as compared to the state-of-the art.
Given its computational efficiency and adaptability, this
framework naturally extends to more complex scenarios.
Future work will generalize the adaptive dictionary framework
to 3-D spatial parameter estimation for applications such as
drone swarm tracking and distributed sensor networks, while
preserving
computational
efficiency
through
noise-aware
region pruning. Furthermore, the method will be extended
to handle irregular array geometries, which are critical for
next-generation
MIMO
radar
systems
requiring
flexible
hardware configurations.
