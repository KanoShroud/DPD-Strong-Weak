[PDF p.1] Abstract—This article proposes a novel 2-D direction-of- arrival (DOA) estimation method based on sparse constraints and adaptive optimization, aiming to address issues such as grid mismatch and high computational complexity in tra- ditional DOA estimation techniques. In contrast to using a traditional grid over distinct angles, combined angle informa- tion atoms are adopted to construct the dictionary, using a power-based covariance matrix fitting method to estimate the noise power and then to select the active regions. To com- pensate for off-grid bias effects, a 2-D dictionary learning procedure is proposed, which is able to yield accurate esti- mates even in single snapshot scenarios, without assuming prior information about the number of sources. Numerical simulations illustrate the preferable performance of the proposed method as compared to recent 2-D DOA estimation algorithms.

[PDF p.1] ETERMINING the azimuth and elevation angles of various forms of reflectors is a commonly occurring problem in a wide range of applications, enabling precise spatial location information [2]. Various classical uniform linear array (ULA) methods have been extended to allow for 2-D direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation, including the classical MUSIC [3] and ESPRIT [4] algorithms. As is well known, the estimation performance of these methods degrades significantly as the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) decreases, when limited data samples are available, when the number of sources is unknown, or when the background noise not suffi- ciently white. These shortcoming has led to the development of several alternative approaches.

[PDF p.1] Recently, sparsity-driven approaches have attracted notable attention as an alternative to enforce sparse solutions. It has been shown that convex relaxation algorithms, such as the LASSO-based DOA estimation algorithms [5], [6], demonstrate excellent performance for grid-based DOA estimation. However, these methods strongly rely on that the source is on the assumed grid, rapidly losing performance as the source moves off the grid. Several other approaches have been proposed. A cross-covariance matrix of an L-shaped sparse array method was proposed in [7] to increase the degrees of freedom (DOF), though it requires a strictly defined array structure. The eigenvalue decomposition-based estimation methods in [8] and [9] and the 2-D grids searching methods in [10] and [11] typically require fine grid resolutions to maintain accuracy [12], [13], as does the Newtonized orthogonal matching pursuit (NOMP) algorithm introduced in [14]. Extending these approaches further, the the CFAR based Newtonized OMP (NOMP-CFAR) algorithm [15] further enhances robustness in line spectral estimation for radar and sonar applications. By incorporating CFAR detection into the iterative Newton refinement process, NOMP-CFAR not only mitigates off-grid bias but also maintains a constant false alarm rate under unknown and varying noise conditions, making it well-suited for practical signal processing tasks.
