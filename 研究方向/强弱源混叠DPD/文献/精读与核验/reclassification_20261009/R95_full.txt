A fast direct locator for radiation source based on
composite convolution neural network

Guomei Zhang,1,✉Guobing Li,1
and Yue Mao2
1School of Information and Communications Engineering, Xi’an Jiao-
tong University, Xi’an, Shaanxi, China
2Xi’an Research Institute of Surveying and Mapping, Xi’an, Shaanxi,
China
✉E-mail: zhanggm@mail.xjtu.edu.cn
The high spatial search complexity of the direct positioning method
in passive positioning systems leads to long positioning time and high
computational resource consumption. In response to this issue, this ar-
ticle proposes a fast localization scheme based on composite convolu-
tional neural networks (CCNN), which can effectively explore the cor-
relation between the position of the radiation source and the charac-
teristics of the received signal. CCNN is a 20-layer composite network
based on fully convolutional network layer, which is composed of con-
volutional layers, batch normalization (BN) layers, and ReLU activation
function layers with unidirectional connections. Then, CCNNs are ad-
justed and trained for positioning single and multiple radiation sources,
respectively. Simulation results show that the computational time of the
proposed method can be reduced by nearly 98% compared with the di-
rect positioning scheme. Meanwhile, about 71.2% of positioning error’s
reduction is achieved.
Introduction: Radiation source (RS) localization technology plays a sig-
niﬁcant role in military and civilian ﬁelds, such as electronic counter-
measure reconnaissance, search and rescue, radio monitoring and shared
transportation. The passive positioning of radiation source has been
widely concerned due to its good concealment, long location distance
and strong survival ability. The related research can be mainly divided
into three categories—two-step localization, direct position determina-
tion and deep learning based localization.
A two-step locator generally includes two steps: parameter extrac-
tion and position calculation. Time of arrival (TDOA), received signal
strength (RSS) and angle of arrival (AOA) are some common local-
ization parameters used in radio source positioning [1]. However, due
to the effect of some factors such as the signal’s phase ambiguity, the
constrained sampling frequency and complex channel environments, the
estimation of these parameters often results in errors and the localiza-
tion performance is further limited. In order to reduce the dependence
of positioning accuracy on some single parameter, multiple parameters
were used jointly in some works. For instance, TDOA and AOA were
used jointly to locate the source by exploring both time-domain and
space-domain information in [2]. For position calculation in a two-step
locator, the weighted least squares method (WLS), two-stage weighted
least squares (TS-WLS), Chan algorithm and Taylor algorithm are usu-
ally adopted to solve the equations based on the selected positioning
parameters. However, these methods cannot compensate for the infor-
mation loss caused by parameter extraction, inevitably leading to limita-
tions in positioning accuracy. Meanwhile, the introduction of intentional
and unintentional noise can also lead to poorer performance of the two-
step method.
To reduce the effect of intermediate parameters estimation on the ﬁ-
nal positioning performance, a direct position determination (DPD) al-
gorithm was proposed by Amar in [3]. In this method, an objective func-
tion is established by directly using the received raw signal. Then, an
exhaustive search is performed throughout the regularly divided space
grids to ﬁnd the optimal value of the objective function. The center po-
sition of the gird with the optimal value is just the localization result. In
order to further improve positioning accuracy, the researchers have been
focusing on how to construct a better cost function to achieve a closer
proximity to the true position of the target during the search process.
Furthermore, various parameters related to the source have been used to
build the cost function, such as Doppler frequency shift [4], TDOA and
FDOA [5]. Since exhaustive search is usually included to optimize the
cost function in many DPD methods, a high computational complexity is
inevitable. In order to reduce computational pressure, distributed direct
positioning schemes [6], majorization-minimization (MM) algorithm [7]
and parallel factor (PARAFAC) analysis [8] were proposed subsequently.
The former disperses the complexity of centralized positioning to differ-
ent distributed devices, while the latter two schemes use optimization
and parallel analysis methods to replace the exhaustive search, respec-
tively. However, these processing will result in the consumption of hard-
ware resources and the loss of positioning accuracy. The DPD method
faces serious drawbacks such as high search complexity and difﬁculty in
establishing cost functions. Therefore, how to balance positioning accu-
racy and computational complexity has become a dilemma.
Recently, deep learning has been introduced into the radio positioning
ﬁeld. In [9], multilayer perceptron (MLP) neural network was adopted to
realize mapping between objective positions and positioning parameters
in two-step method for indoor WiFi-based localization. However, deep
neural networks often require a large number of fully connected rela-
tionships, which puts great pressure on real-time computation and pre-
training of nodes, making it difﬁcult to achieve the real-time superiority
of advanced optimization methods as described in [10–12]. The scheme
based on LSTM network is introduced in the context of ultra wide band
(UWB) technology, whose relative positioning error can reach 1.4%
[13], but the disadvantage is also high complexity. Fortunately, recent re-
search used a combination of convolutional neural network (CNN) and
SVM in the task of plant disease classiﬁcation [14], which improved
classiﬁcation accuracy while signiﬁcantly reducing computational com-
plexity. Therefore, CNN-based deep learning networks are more suitable
for preserving important features and reducing complexity.
The two-step localization method and the direct localization method
face the bottlenecks of low localization accuracy and high computational
complexity, respectively. The outstanding contribution of the reference
[15] inspires us that deep learning technology is a way to overcoming
this dilemma and achieving better trade-offs. This issue is handled in
our work. CNN can extract space features contained in data excellently
with a lower processing complexity, it is introduced into our design.
Also a basic unit FCN of the network layer is designed to help build a
lightweight network that can quickly achieve positioning. Different from
the existing deep learning based positioning solutions, which only apply
deep learning networks to extract localization parameters or to replace
localization equations solving, here a composite convolutional neural
network (CCNN) is designed to realize the automatic feature extraction
from the original received data ﬁrst and then directly output positions
of the target sources. It is aimed to minimize complexity while ensuring
superior localization performance. The main contributions of our work
are as follows:
• A fully convolutional network (FCN) block was designed, which in-
cludes a one-dimensional convolutional layer, a normalization layer,
and an activation function layer. This block centers around the convo-
lutional layer and simultaneously achieves feature extraction, numer-
ical normalization, and non-linear feature activation. The moderate
combination of FCN can achieve fast and efﬁcient source localization.
• A composite CNN combing four FCN blocks and four Max-pooling
layers is designed to realize the directly positioning task for single ra-
dio source and multiple radio sources, respectively. Three relevant pa-
rameters for each FCN block are appropriately selected by a lot of re-
peated experiments, which successfully realize high-precision source
localization under low computational complexity conditions.
• Dataset for training and test is established through simulation. Then,
the positioning performances of the trained networks are evaluated un-
der different scenarios by simulation. The results demonstrate that the
proposed locator has strong robustness under the scenes with small-
scale fading and low signal-to-noise ratio. The designed network can
be easily expanded to the case of multi-source localization just by im-
proving the dimension of the output layer. What’s more, the proposed
localization system does not need an antenna array at the sensor com-
pared with the classic multiple signal classiﬁcation (MUSIC)-DPD
method for positioning multiple sources.

Due to the combination of one-dimensional convolutional (1D-CNN)
layers, batch normalization (BN) layers, and activation function layers
being frequently used in classiﬁcation and object detection tasks, it is
also introduced into our network. For convenience, the cascading of the
above three objects is deﬁned as a basic unit and denoted by a full con-
volutional layer (FCN). In a FCN block, 1D-CNN layer is to extract use-
ful features from the input data sequence for the subsequent regression
task. It performs convolution operations on the input data by sliding con-
volutional kernels. BN layer helps to improve the training stability and
performance of neural networks by alleviating the problems of gradient
disappearance and gradient explosion. Activation functions are mainly
used to introduce nonlinear characteristics and can enable neural net-
works to learn and represent complex functional relationships. ReLU
function only involves simple threshold operations, which can improve
the training speed of network and is also robust to gradient explosion.
Therefore, it is selected as the activation function in a FCN layer. The
speciﬁc operation process is shown in the lower part of Figure 2.

Simulation results: In order to test the effectiveness of the proposed
scheme, the system parameters and the network parameters shown in
Table 1 are used in simulation. Radiation sources are randomly gener-
ated in a square area of [300, 300](m) size to obtain the samples for
training and testing network. Based on the counterintuitive phenomenon
of stochastic resonance [16, 17], we added Gaussian white noise to the
training samples to suppress noise and improve system performance.
The locations of RSs during simulation will be taken as the output la-
bels for training network.
Our main experimental assumption is that there are no special ob-
stacles between the radiation source and receiver in the channel, only
with LOS path propagation and large-scale fading path-loss. At present,
complex channel effects such as shadow fading and frequency selective
fading are not being considered. The positioning situation in small-scale
fading channels will be discussed separately in a section of the experi-
ment. For the loss function for training network is the mean square error
(MSE) function. While the evaluation function for testing network is the
mean average error (MAE) function. The deﬁnitions of MSE, MAE and
signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) are same as in reference [2]. For the MSE
loss function, L2 regularization penalty term is added to the square of
the model weights to prevent overﬁtting of model training. Moreover,
samples with a uniformly distributed SNR from [0 dB–25 dB] are taken
as the test set.
The baseline schemes compared with the proposed methods include
the state-of-the-art two-step method using TDOA and AOA jointly [18],
as well as the ML-DPD method with different grid edge lengths that pur-
sue extreme positioning performance. The two-step method combines
time difference and AOA to establish a nonlinear equation system, and
solves it using the WLS method, while the DPD method includes a spa-
tial grid search process based on the maximum likelihood criterion. But
in order to estimate AOA parameters, the two-step method assumes that
each receiver is equipped with four antennas to form a uniform linear
antenna array, which will greatly increase the hardware cost of the re-
ceiver.

In order to verify the robustness of the network after training to the
small-scale fading of channel, the βlp in Equation (1) is selected to fol-
low the Rice fading model [19] when generating testing samples. In Rice
model, K is the energy factor and represents the ratio of transmission
energy between the line-of-sight (LOS) path and the other non-line-of-
sight (NLOS) paths in wireless channel. When K approaches 0, it is a
Rayleigh fading channel. While K approaches positive inﬁnity, the chan-
nel is a Gaussian channel. The simulation results are shown in Figure 4.
It can be seen that CCNN exhibits strong robustness to the small-scale
fading. For the additional Rice fading introduced in the testing samples
(when K = 1), its positioning error only increases by about 10%. How-
ever, for the case of rayleigh fading channel without LOS path, the posi-
tioning error becomes higher than the Rice channel, but still signiﬁcantly
better than the DPD method in the same condition. Based on our basic
experiments and additional experiments introducing Rice fading, we can
conclude that there is a strong mapping relationship between the signal
and position in the passive positioning system. This is because CCNN is
essentially a mathematical model with a ﬁxed calculation process, but it
can adapt well to changes in the position of the radiation source and rela-
tively simple changes in the channel environment. This indicates that the
mapping relationship between the signal and position is likely to exist,
which has been well learned by CCNN.

Conclusions: In response to the high complexity problem of DPD,
a composite convolutional neural network based deep learning local-
ization algorithm is proposed in this paper. Full convolutional layer
(FCN) is adopted as the basic unit of CCNN. The designed and trained
CCNN can learn the positional features contained in the original
signals and deduce the positions of RSs directly from them. Simulation
results show that CCNN can locate radiation sources precisely with
