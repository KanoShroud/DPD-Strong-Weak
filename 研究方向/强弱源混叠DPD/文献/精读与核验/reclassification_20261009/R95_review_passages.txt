[PDF p.1] The high spatial search complexity of the direct positioning method in passive positioning systems leads to long positioning time and high computational resource consumption. In response to this issue, this ar- ticle proposes a fast localization scheme based on composite convolu- tional neural networks (CCNN), which can effectively explore the cor- relation between the position of the radiation source and the charac- teristics of the received signal. CCNN is a 20-layer composite network based on fully convolutional network layer, which is composed of con- volutional layers, batch normalization (BN) layers, and ReLU activation function layers with unidirectional connections. Then, CCNNs are ad- justed and trained for positioning single and multiple radiation sources, respectively. Simulation results show that the computational time of the proposed method can be reduced by nearly 98% compared with the di- rect positioning scheme. Meanwhile, about 71.2% of positioning error’s reduction is achieved.

[PDF p.1] Introduction: Radiation source (RS) localization technology plays a sig- niﬁcant role in military and civilian ﬁelds, such as electronic counter- measure reconnaissance, search and rescue, radio monitoring and shared transportation. The passive positioning of radiation source has been widely concerned due to its good concealment, long location distance and strong survival ability. The related research can be mainly divided into three categories—two-step localization, direct position determina- tion and deep learning based localization.

[PDF p.1] A two-step locator generally includes two steps: parameter extrac- tion and position calculation. Time of arrival (TDOA), received signal strength (RSS) and angle of arrival (AOA) are some common local- ization parameters used in radio source positioning [1]. However, due to the effect of some factors such as the signal’s phase ambiguity, the constrained sampling frequency and complex channel environments, the estimation of these parameters often results in errors and the localiza- tion performance is further limited. In order to reduce the dependence of positioning accuracy on some single parameter, multiple parameters were used jointly in some works. For instance, TDOA and AOA were used jointly to locate the source by exploring both time-domain and space-domain information in [2]. For position calculation in a two-step locator, the weighted least squares method (WLS), two-stage weighted least squares (TS-WLS), Chan algorithm and Taylor algorithm are usu- ally adopted to solve the equations based on the selected positioning parameters. However, these methods cannot compensate for the infor- mation loss caused by parameter extraction, inevitably leading to limita- tions in positioning accuracy. Meanwhile, the introduction of intentional and unintentional noise can also lead to poorer performance of the two- step method.

[PDF p.1] To reduce the effect of intermediate parameters estimation on the ﬁ- nal positioning performance, a direct position determination (DPD) al- gorithm was proposed by Amar in [3]. In this method, an objective func- tion is established by directly using the received raw signal. Then, an exhaustive search is performed throughout the regularly divided space grids to ﬁnd the optimal value of the objective function. The center po- sition of the gird with the optimal value is just the localization result. In order to further improve positioning accuracy, the researchers have been focusing on how to construct a better cost function to achieve a closer proximity to the true position of the target during the search process. Furthermore, various parameters related to the source have been used to build the cost function, such as Doppler frequency shift [4], TDOA and FDOA [5].
