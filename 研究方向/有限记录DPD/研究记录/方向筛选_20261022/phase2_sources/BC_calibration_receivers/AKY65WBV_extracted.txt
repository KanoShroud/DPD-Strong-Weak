ABSTRACT:
This paper presents a novel self-calibration direct position determination (SC-DPD) method in asynchronous sensor networks without the aid of anchor sources. By leveraging the consistency of asynchronous clock bias across different intercepted signal batches, the localization cost function is derived to effectively mitigate asynchronous effects. Simulation results demonstrate enhanced performance of the proposed method over existing studies: (1) superior localization accuracy, particularly in low Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) conditions, (2) lower system complexity through anchor-free operation, (3) reduced computational complexity without clock bias estimation in the asynchronous sensor networks. The proposed method bridges the gap between theoretical DPD frameworks and practical deployment constraints, particularly for large-scale sensor networks.

INTRODUCTION Passive localization of non-cooperative sources is increasingly attractive for applications including smart cities, autonomous systems and industrial automation [1–3]. Common localization methods comprise two main categories: two-step methods and direct position determination (DPD) methods. In two-step methods, the parameters, such as Time/Frequency Difference of Arrival (TDOA/FDOA), Angle of Arrival (AOA), are first estimated and then utilized to calculate the source position. In low Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) conditions, the accuracy significantly degrades. To address this limitation, DPD methods are proposed by determining the position directly from intercepted signals without estimating intermediate parameters, enhancing robustness at the cost of significantly increased computational complexity. Most existing research is for synchronous sensor networks. However, in practical applications, the manufacturing †Co-first author, ⋆Corresponding author E-mail address: zhangyuan@nudt.edu.cn, yaozihan19@nudt.edu.cn, wuguizhou@nudt.edu.cn, gfcly75@163.com, lixi16@nudt.edu.cn, zhangmin1984@126.com error of the microwave RF front-ends, the propagation of signals through inhomogeneous media, and even the interruption of timing reference signals (e.g., GPS) can cause unknown clock biases, which prevent satisfying the synchronous assumption [1, 2, 4–7]. This paper proposes a self-calibration DPD (SC-DPD) method for asynchronous sensor networks to address RF propagation phenomena and hardware-related imperfections. Based on the consistency of clock bias, the sources are localized without using anchor sources or estimating clock bias. The main contributions are summarized below. • Compared with the existing two-step methods [4,8,9], the proposed SC-DPD can directly construct the cost function by intercepted signals, which avoids information loss in suboptimal two-step estimation and enhances the performance especially in low SNR ratio conditions. • Compared with existing DPD methods in [2, 10–12], which require the anchor sources with known positions and the front-end RF receiving circuits to distinguish the unknown source signal and the anchor source signal, the proposed SC-DPD avoids these problems without introducing anchor sources and effectively reduces the framework complexity. • Compared with the joint clock bias and position estimation methods [13–15], SC-DPD utilizes the consistency of clock biases to avoid estimating high-dimensional clock bias vectors, which significantly decreases computational demands and performs better especially for large-scale sensors network scenarios. Computational Complexity Analysis To further clarify the computational complexity-related capabilities of the proposed algorithm, this subsection conducts comparative analysis with other methods from both theoretical analysis and simulation experiments. Simulation conditions follow those described in Section4.1.The theoretical computational complexity and simulation results of different localization methods are shown in Table.1. The two-step method exploits the consistency of asynchronous clock biases to formulate positioning equations, with computational complexity primarily arising from matrix inversion, resulting in low computational burden. The grid-search asynchronous DPD method is (M − 1 + d)dimensional joint search for clock biases and source position. When search range is large or high estimation accuracy is required, the computational cost grows exponentially. The anchor DPD method utilizes EM and Gaussian-Newton iteration to reduce the estimation complexity, with computational cost primarily determined by EM-based initialization. The proposed method exploits the consistency of asynchronous clock biases across different intercept batches to eliminate their effects without estimating the bias values. Theoretical analysis and simulation results reveals that while SC-DPD requires longer computational time than the two-step method, its complexity is limited to d-dimensional estimation, representing a substantial reduction compared to other DPD methods. Thus, SC-DPD is particularly suitable for large-scale asynchronous sensor networks. Moreover, the additional computational burden relative to traditional synchronous-DPD methods remains negligible, thereby preserving real-time processing capabilities. 5. CONCLUSION A novel self-calibration DPD method in asynchronous sensor networks for the sources is proposed without the aid of anchor sources, in which the slow-changing feature of clock biases is utilized to eliminate their influence rather than estimating them. Compared with the existing researches, the proposed method realizes lower computational cost and framework complexity especially for large-scale sensor networks, thus improves real-time performance significantly. 21599 Authorized licensed use limited to: XIDIAN UNIVERSITY.
