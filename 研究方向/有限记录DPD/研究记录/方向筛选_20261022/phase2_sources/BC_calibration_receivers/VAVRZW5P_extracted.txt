ABSTRACT:
The direct position determination (DPD) approach has been shown to be superior to alternative (two-steps) indirect methods for emitter localization in terms of the resulting accuracy. However, its requirement of joint raw data processing from all receiving non-co-located sensor arrays in different base stations results in one of its main drawbacks-that (all the) raw data be transferred to a common processor. To alleviate the communication requirements between the base stations, it has been recently proposed to use a coarsely quantized version of the raw data, specifically with one-bit-per-sample quantized measurements, for which the One-Bit DPD (OBDPD) method was developed. While OBDPD significantly reduces the requirements on the communication links between the base stations, similarly to classical DPD, it typically suffers from (substantially) degraded localization accuracy when multiple emitters are present in the area of interest. To tackle this limitation, we propose high-resolution OBDPD (HROBDPD), which is similar in nature to OBDPD, but with a minimum variance distortionless response approach. Despite the one-bit-per-sample quantization, HROB-DPD yields favorable spatial heat maps for the multiple-emitters case, thus leading to enhanced localization. Simulation results demonstrate the improved performance of HROBDPD relative to OBDPD in a scenario with more than a single emitter.

INTRODUCTION Emitter localization, a pivotal problem in signal processing with applications spanning autonomous vehicles, radar, lidar, sonar systems, and battlefield surveillance, has been extensively addressed in the literature, e.g., [1]–[8]. Various methods typically involve a two-step approach (i.e., indirect localization) that initially estimate a related statistic, e.g., timeof-arrival and/or angle-of-arrival at different base stations, followed by a second position estimation step, based on these previously estimated statistics. However, from a statistical optimality standpoint, these approaches overlook the informative constraint that the measurements from all base station share the same signal emitted from the same emitter (obviously, at the same position). To exploit this, the (one-step) direct position determination (DPD) method has been proposed in [9], followed by a variety of variants and extensions [10]–[16]. Hence, it introduces a trade off between localization accuracy and communication1 resources. This localization problem is illustrated in Fig. 1. To alleviate the communication requirements between the base stations, and strike a better balance in the aforementioned trade off, the One-Bit DPD (OBDPD) method [17] has been proposed, in which one-bit-per-sample quantized signals are used for direct localization. While OBDPD serves as a competitive method in terms of the balance between estimation accuracy vs. communication resource, it is stable (in its performance) only for localization of a single emitter, and lacks robustness in this sense. Building on OBDPD and the previously proposed HR DPD for multiple emitters [18], our main contribution in this work is a HR version of OBDPD (HROBDPD). Similarly to OBDPD, the proposed method requires only that one-bit-per-sample quantized versions of the received signals to be sent from the base stations to the central processor. It provides a spatial “heat map”2 that allows to identify more accurately multiple emitters in the area of interest, thus addressing the limitation of OBDPD for the (quite plausible) multiple-emitter scenario. While the method is developed under the Gaussian signal model assumption, we demonstrate through simulation that it can be successfully applied to non-Gaussian signals as well, which broadens the potential applicability of our method. CONCLUDING REMARKS We propose the high-resolution one-bit direct position determination (HROBDPD) method aimed at improving localization accuracy in multi-emitter scenarios, while minimizing communication requirements between the the base stations to the central computing unit. Building on the previously proposed one-bit DPD framework, HROBDPD leverages an MVDR approach to create accurate spatial heat maps for emitter localization. Simulation results demonstrate that HROBDPD achieves significantly improved localization accuracy over OBDPD when there is more than a single emitter, while still requiring from the distant, non-co-located base stations to communicate only one-bit-per-sample quantized signals. Future work is focused on further developing new quantization strategies (such as [21]), so as to obtain a better trade off between communication efficiency and localization accuracy. Combining the MVDR approach with improved quantization strategies could potentially lead to even more attractive, practical methods for real-world, multi-emitter localization tasks. REFERENCES [1]A.
