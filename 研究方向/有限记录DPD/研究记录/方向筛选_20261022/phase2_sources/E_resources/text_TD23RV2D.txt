Correspondence Direct Position Determination Using Compressive Sensing Measurements Without Reconstruction This article proposes a direct position determination (DPD) method for stationary emitters with compressed signal measurements. Employing Hadamard matrix properties, the method estimates the source location directly in the compressing measurement domain, without the step of signal reconstruction and time-differences-ofarrival parameter extraction. In the case of free-space propagation, an augmented DPD method is proposed to further utilize the positionrelated received signal strength information. In addition, the Cramér– Rao lower bounds for the emitter position of both DPD methods are derived for performance evaluation. I. INTRODUCTION The problem of locating a source through its emitted signals has been of considerable interest to both academic researchers and engineering practitioners [1], [2], [3], [4], [5], [6], [7], [8]. Most emitter localization methods consist of two steps. In the first step, the position-related parameters are extracted through the signals of several receivers. Then, in the second step, the estimated emitter’s position should best fit the collection of the extracted parameters. In the first step, the time-differences-of-arrival (TDOA) is preferable as a generally accurate position estimation that can be achieved through several time-synchronized one-channel receivers [9], [10]. However, such merit of the TDOA-based localization method is obtained usually at the cost of large inter-receiver communication bandwidth. To address the issue, many studies have been conducted to make the TDOA-based localization methods feasible in case in which the communication resources are restricted, among which compressive sensing (CS) techniques are commonly used [11], [12], [13]. A standard CS problem consists of two steps: signal compressing and signal reconstruction. In the signal compressing step, a compressing matrix is designed to compress the original measurements into the measurements of much lower dimensions [14]. Then, in the signal reconstruction step, efforts are made to recover the original measurements [15], [16], [17]. However, the signal reconstruction step may induce a great computation cost and is not preferable for real-time applications. To handle the issue, Salari et al. The method in [18] is proved to be superior to the orthogonal matching pursuit reconstruction method [19] in terms of TDOA estimation accuracy and the focal underdetermined system solver reconstruction method [20] in terms of processing time. Generally, CS-based localization approaches perform well at high signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs). However, their performance degrades rapidly when SNR becomes low, which is very severe in the case of high CS rates. In practice, a promising technique to handle rapid performance degradation in low SNRs is the direct position determination (DPD) paradigm [21], [22], [23], [24], [25], [26], [27], [28]. Different from the two-step methods, a DPD method locates the emitter directly from the received signals. A DPD method can utilize the underlying restriction that all observations should be consistent with the same emitter. In addition, the process of parameter covariance estimation is avoided, which may sometimes be difficult to handle. To improve the performance of CS-based localization methods, especially at low SNRs, this article is devoted to an effective integration of CS and DPD techniques, which has not yet been reported to our best knowledge. Specifically, employing Hadamard matrix properties, we develop a one-step CS-based DPD method that eliminates the complex reconstruction step and the TDOA parameter extraction step, and estimates the source location directly in the compressive measurement domain. In addition, an augmented DPD method is proposed to further improve the localization accuracy in case of free-space propagation by utilizing the position-related information in received signal strength (RSS). The Cramér–Rao lower bounds (CRLBs) for the emitter position of both DPD methods are derived. Our simulation results show that the proposed CS-based DPD method performs comparably with the classical DPD method without CS when SNR is high. By choosing a reasonable compressing rate, the CS-based DPD method can also perform well when SNR is low. When the emitted signals are subject to free-space propagation, better performance can be achieved by the RSS-augmented CS-based DPD method. II. SYSTEM MODEL Consider a stationary source located at u = [x, y, z]T and L stationary receivers located at pl = [xl, yl, zl ]T, l = 1, 2,..., L. The source starts emitting signals at time t = 0. The receivers are time synchronized. The time the signals take to propagate from the source to receiver l is τl = ‖u − pl ‖/ c (1) where c is the speed of wave propagation. Given that the source signal is s(t ) at time t, then the signal received by receiver l at time t can be modeled as zl (t ) = al (t ) s (t − τl ) + wl (t ) (2) where al (t ) is the path attenuation factor and is usually approximately stationary over the signal observing period. Consequently, we can assume al (t ) ≈ al. The noise term wl (t ) is zero mean and statistically independent circular complex Gaussian noise such that E {w2 1 (t )} = σ 2 l. In prac tice, the continuous noise term wl (t ) is mainly generated due to the thermal vibration of electrons of analog devices and analog-to-digital converters, and the dominant component is usually from the low-noise amplifiers. For the case of using the same type of receivers, which is common in practice, we assume σ 2 1 = ··· = σ2 L = σ2 w in this article. Note that it is straightforward to extend to heterogeneous noise levels [29]. However, Salari et al. [18] show that it is possible to retain the time-shift relationship if a Hadamard compressing matrix is used. A Hadamard matrix of order N = 2n(n = 1, 2, 3...), denoted by HN, is an N × N matrix whose elements are either +1 or −1 and its rows are mutually orthogonal [18]. It should be noted that the Hadamard matrix can only be used for compressing signals with dimension N = 2n, M = 2m(n, m = 1, 2, 3,..., m < n). NUMERICAL INVESTIGATIONS This section conducts several numerical experiments to evaluate the performance of the proposed DPD methods. We consider a scenario where one stationary source and three fixed receivers are present and the signals propagate in free space. The source is located at u = [14.5, 102.6, 10]T (km) with the known altitude. The positions of the receivers are p1 = [40, 1, 10]T (km), p2 = [0, 0, 10]T (km), and p3 = [0, 80, 10]T (km), respectively. The source emits signals with BPSK modulation and the bandwidth is 25 kHz. The carrier frequency is set to be fc = 403 MHz and the sampling frequency is fs = 100 kHz. A total of 1024 sampling points are collected per source location. For simplicity and without the loss of generality, we assume κ = 1. To gain more insights into the proposed DPD methods, three benchmarks are considered. The first benchmark is the classical DPD method without CS. The second benchmark is the two-step localization method based on the compressed measurements as considered in [18]. And the third benchmark is the two-step localization method based on the original uncompressed measurements. CONCLUSION We have developed a CS-based DPD method that eliminates both the complex reconstruction step and the TDOA parameter extraction step. The method can estimate the source location directly in the compressive measurement domain. In addition, an RSS-augmented DPD method is proposed to further improve the localization accuracy when signals are subject to free-space propagation. The CRLBs for the emitter position of both DPD methods are derived for performance evaluation. The proposed CS-based DPD method performs comparably with the classical DPD method without CS at high SNRs. With a reasonable compressing rate, the CS-based DPD method can also perform well when SNR is low. When the emitted signals are subject to free-space propagation, better performance can be achieved by the RSS-augmented CS-based DPD method.
